LESSON 07 — FOOD & ORDERING

Lesson goals:
  • Talk about food and meals: el desayuno, la comida, la cena.
  • Use querer (to want) with a noun or an infinitive.
  • Say you are hungry or thirsty with tener hambre / tener sed.
  • Order in a café or restaurant and ask for la cuenta.

Core vocabulary: querer, comida, desayuno, almuerzo, cena, agua, pan, fruta, verdura, carne, pescado, restaurante, mercado, camarero/a, carta, cuenta, mesa, plato, rico/rica, tener hambre, tener sed, para mí
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): sopa, patatas, vino, postre, pagar, traer, preguntar, al final, tomar, antojitos, probar

1. READING PASSAGE (A SATURDAY MARKET)

Context: Marta tells us about a normal Saturday with her father. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Read top to bottom — the Spanish is enough to get the gist.)

Me llamo Marta y vivo en Valencia.

My name is Marta and I live in Valencia.

Hoy es sábado y voy al mercado con mi padre.

Today is Saturday and I'm going to the market with my father.

En el mercado hay mucha comida: fruta, verdura, pan y pescado.

At the market there's a lot of food: fruit, vegetables, bread and fish.

Mi padre quiere pescado para la cena.

My father wants fish for dinner.

Después vamos a un restaurante pequeño y el camarero trae la carta.

Then we go to a small restaurant and the waiter brings the menu.

Yo quiero una sopa y mi padre quiere carne con patatas.

I want a soup and my father wants meat with potatoes.

Bebemos agua porque no bebemos vino.

We drink water because we don't drink wine.

El camarero pregunta: «¿Quieren postre?» — «Sí, una fruta, por favor.»

The waiter asks: "Would you like dessert?" — "Yes, a fruit, please."

Al final, pagamos y volvemos a casa. ¡La comida está rica!

In the end, we pay and go back home. The food is delicious!

💡 Memory hook: Food words are easy to live with. In a restaurant you only need three moves: Quiero… (I want), ¿Me trae…? (Could you bring me…?), La cuenta, por favor (The bill, please).

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. querer — to want

querer is one of the most useful verbs in daily life. It means "to want" (and, with people, "to love"). Use it with a noun or with an infinitive:

querer + noun  ·  querer + infinitive
PersonquererEnglish
yoquieroI want
túquieresyou want
él / ella / ustedquierehe / she wants
nosotrosqueremoswe want
vosotros (Spain)queréisyou all want
ellos / ustedesquierenthey / you all want
  • Quiero una sopa, por favor.I want a soup, please.
  • ¿Quieres un café?Do you want a coffee?
  • Queremos aprender español.We want to learn Spanish.
⚠️ Notice the change: the e of querer becomes ie in most forms: quiero, quieres, quiere, quieren — but queremos, queréis stay regular. You will study this pattern in Lesson 10.

2.2. tener hambre / tener sed

In Spanish you don't "be" hungry — you "have" hunger. Use tener (Lesson 03) + a noun:

tener hambre = to be hungry · tener sed = to be thirsty
  • Tengo hambre. ¿Y tú?I'm hungry. And you?
  • El niño tiene sed.The boy is thirsty.
  • ¿Tienes hambre o quieres beber algo?Are you hungry or do you want to drink something?
💡 Same pattern, other feelings: tener sueño (to be sleepy), tener frío (to be cold), tener calor (to be hot), tener miedo (to be afraid).

2.3. Ordering food and drink

A few phrases carry you through any café. Read them as whole chunks:

  • ¿Qué desea?What would you like? (formal; the waiter asks you)
  • Para mí, una sopa.For me, a soup.
  • De primero, una ensalada; de segundo, pescado.As a starter, a salad; as a main, fish. (advanced)
  • ¿Me trae la carta, por favor?Could you bring me the menu, please?
  • La cuenta, por favor.The bill, please.
💡 Spain vs Latin America: the bill is la cuenta everywhere. The waiter is el camarero in Spain, but el mesero or el mozo in much of Latin America.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences you can use from day one. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Say you're hungry:

Tengo hambre. ¿Quieres comer algo?

I'm hungry. Do you want to eat something?

2. Order simply:

Para mí, una sopa y agua, por favor.

For me, a soup and water, please.

3. Ask for the menu:

¿Me trae la carta, por favor?

Could you bring me the menu, please?

4. Compliment the food:

La comida está muy rica.

The food is very delicious.

5. Pay and leave:

La cuenta, por favor. Gracias.

The bill, please. Thank you.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Marta and her father order lunch in a small restaurant.

Camarero: Buenas tardes. ¿Qué desean?

Waiter: Good afternoon. What would you like?

Marta: Para mí, una sopa y una ensalada.

Marta: For me, a soup and a salad.

Padre: Yo quiero carne con patatas.

Father: I want meat with potatoes.

Camarero: ¿Para beber?

Waiter: To drink?

Marta: Agua, por favor. ¿Y tú, papá?

Marta: Water, please. And you, Dad?

Padre: Agua también. No bebemos vino.

Father: Water too. We don't drink wine.

Camarero: ¡Muy bien! Ahora traigo la comida.

Waiter: Very good! I'll bring the food now.

New words in the dialogue: ensalada (salad), ahora (now).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • querer = to want: quiero, quieres, quiere… + noun or infinitive.
  • To be hungry / thirsty, use tener: tengo hambre, tienes sed.
  • Ordering: ¿Qué desea? / Para mí… / La cuenta, por favor.
  • Meals: el desayuno, la comida / el almuerzo, la cena.
  • camarero (Spain) vs mesero / mozo (Latin America).

5.2. Further reading — tapas and antojitos

Context: A short cultural note on small plates. Read it quietly:

En España, es muy común ir de tapas con los amigos.

In Spain, it's very common to go for tapas with friends.

Las tapas son platos pequeños: aceitunas, tortilla, jamón, queso.

Tapas are small dishes: olives, Spanish omelette, ham, cheese.

En México, los antojitos son la versión local: tacos, quesadillas, tamales.

In Mexico, antojitos are the local version: tacos, quesadillas, tamales.

La idea es la misma: comida pequeña, mucha conversación y buen tiempo.

The idea is the same: small food, lots of conversation and a good time.

¿Tienes hambre ahora? ¡Vamos a comer!

Are you hungry now? Let's go eat!

New words: tapa (small plate), aceituna (olive), tortilla (omelette), jamón (ham), queso (cheese), taco (taco), quesadilla (cheese tortilla), tamal (tamale).
