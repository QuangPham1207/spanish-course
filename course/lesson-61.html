LESSON 61 — NON-FINITE CLAUSES

Lesson goals:
  • Read absolute participles: terminada la restauración…, una vez desmontado…
  • Use the infinitive as a noun: restaurar bien lleva tiempo, fumar es malo…
  • Read gerund clauses — including the advanced habiéndose… form.
  • Follow a restoration workshop bringing a church altarpiece back to life.
  • Pick up words: retablo, barniz, pátina, ebanista, andamio, dorado.

Core vocabulary: restauración, restaurar, marco, barniz, grieta, andamio, fresco, altar, viga, escombro, artesano, ebanista, dorado, bastidor, carpintero, retablo, vitral, cantero, lija, lijar, desmontar, catalogar, documentar, diagnóstico, consolidar, reponer, pátina, craquelado, meticuloso, aprendiz, pulir, policromía, fumar, pintura, polvo, cliente, calidad
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): decapar, reintegración, impoluto

1. READING PASSAGE (TERMINADA LA RESTAURACIÓN)

Context: A small restoration workshop in Puebla spends four months on a church altarpiece. The chronicle is full of participles standing alone, infinitives acting as nouns, and gerunds carrying whole scenes.

(Non-finite = a verb with no person and no tense: the participle, the infinitive and the gerund doing the work of whole clauses.)

Terminada la restauración del altar, la iglesia del pueblo volvió a abrir sus puertas.

With the restoration of the altar finished, the village church opened its doors again.

El retablo, una pintura del siglo dieciocho sobre madera, llevaba doscientos años lleno de polvo.

The altarpiece, an 18th-century painting on wood, had spent two hundred years covered in dust.

El primer trabajo fue el diagnóstico: catalogar todo y documentar cada grieta del retablo durante dos semanas.

The first job was the diagnosis: cataloguing everything and documenting every crack in the altarpiece for two weeks.

Una vez desmontado el retablo, los artesanos subieron al andamio con lija y pinceles.

Once the altarpiece was taken apart, the craftspeople climbed the scaffolding with sandpaper and brushes.

Lijar a mano es lento, y el aprendiz, meticuloso, pasó una semana entera con la lija en la mano.

Sanding by hand is slow, and the apprentice — meticulous — spent a whole week with the sandpaper in his hand.

El carpintero tuvo que reponer una viga comida por los años y consolidar el bastidor del marco.

The carpenter had to replace a beam eaten away by the years and consolidate the frame's inner stretcher.

El ebanista limpió el dorado con agua y tiempo, cuidando la pátina de tres siglos.

The cabinetmaker cleaned the gilding with water and patience, taking care of the three-centuries-old patina.

De cerca, la policromía mostraba un craquelado fino, lleno de pequeñas grietas.

Up close, the polychromy showed a fine craquelure, full of tiny cracks.

Restaurar es decidir: el fresco del altar se quedó como estaba.

To restore is to decide: the fresco on the altar stayed as it was.

El lijar y el pulir llevaron todo el mes de mayo.

The sanding and the polishing took up the whole month of May.

El cliente dice que fumar es malo para la pintura.

The client says that smoking is bad for the painting.

El vitral del coro se limpió con agua clara, poco a poco.

The choir's stained-glass window was cleaned with clear water, little by little.

Lijando el marco con cuidado, el aprendiz encontró un año escrito a mano: 1798.

While sanding the frame carefully, the apprentice found a year written by hand: 1798.

Limpio ya el barniz viejo, los colores parecían nuevos.

With the old varnish now cleaned off, the colours looked new.

El cantero llevó el escombro fuera de la iglesia, en tres viajes.

The stonemason carried the rubble out of the church in three trips.

Al final, el cliente pagó el precio justo por un trabajo de gran calidad.

In the end, the client paid the fair price for a job of great quality.

El dorado, ya pulido a mano, parece nuevo.

The gilding, now hand-polished, looks new.

Terminada la obra, el pueblo entró a mirar: el retablo parecía otro.

With the work finished, the village came in to look: the altarpiece seemed a different one.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Absolute participles: terminada la restauración…

An absolute construction is a participle that agrees with a noun, followed by the main clause — with no connector like cuando or porque. Una vez often introduces it. It is the signature opening of chronicles and reports.

Participle (agrees: terminar → terminada/terminados) + noun + main clause
  • Terminada la restauración, la iglesia volvió a abrir.With the restoration finished, the church reopened.
  • Una vez desmontado el retablo, subieron al andamio.Once the altarpiece was taken apart, they climbed the scaffolding.
  • Limpio ya el barniz, los colores parecían nuevos.With the varnish now cleaned, the colours looked new.
  • Documentada cada grieta, empezó el trabajo.With every crack documented, the work began. (feminine: documentada)

2.2. The infinitive as a noun: restaurar es decidir

A Spanish infinitive works as a masculine singular noun — subject, or object with el. No plural, no article changes: el lijar y el pulir (the sanding and the polishing).

Infinitive = masculine singular noun: restaurar es decidir · fumar es malo · el lijar y el pulir
  • Restaurar es decidir.To restore is to decide. (infinitive as subject)
  • Lijar a mano es lento.Sanding by hand is slow.
  • El cliente dice que fumar es malo.The client says smoking is bad.
  • El lijar y el pulir llevaron mayo.The sanding and polishing took up May. (with article: el + infinitive)
💡 Spain-vs-LatAm note: these constructions are identical everywhere. You will meet absolute participles most in writing from Spain's press and in Latin American formal reports alike — it is a written-register tool, not a regional one.

2.3. Gerund clauses: lijando… / habiéndose… (advanced)

A gerund clause sets the background — "while / by / after …-ing". The advanced form habiéndose + participle marks something already finished when the main action starts; read it for recognition only.

Gerund + main clause (lijar → lijando…) · (advanced) terminar → habiéndose terminado…
  • Lijando el marco, encontró el año.While sanding the frame, he found the year. (= mientras lijaba)
  • Cuidando la pátina, limpió el dorado.Taking care of the patina, she cleaned the gilding.
  • Estando rota la viga, no se pudo seguir.The beam being broken, they couldn't go on. (advanced absolute gerund)
  • Habiéndose terminado la obra, cerraron el taller.The work having been finished, they closed the workshop. (advanced)
⚠️ Common mistakes: a gerund can never be the subject: lijar es lento (right), not lijando es lento (wrong — use the infinitive). And an absolute participle must agree: terminada la obra, documentada cada grieta.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five workshop sentences. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. The opener:

Terminada la restauración, abrimos la iglesia.

With the restoration finished, we open the church.

2. The motto:

Restaurar bien lleva tiempo.

Restoring well takes time.

3. The rule:

Fumar es malo para la pintura.

Smoking is bad for the painting.

4. The lesson:

Lijando se aprende.

You learn by sanding.

5. The bill:

El precio es justo por esta calidad.

The price is fair for this quality.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: The client visits the workshop to see how his altarpiece is doing.

Cliente: ¿Cuándo terminan la restauración del retablo?

Client: When will the restoration of the altarpiece be finished?

Lucía: Terminada la reintegración, solo falta el barniz.

Lucía: Once the infilling is done, only the varnish is missing.

Cliente: ¿Ese color es el de 1798?

Client: Is that colour the one from 1798?

Lucía: Sí. Decapamos la pintura nueva y salió el color de 1798.

Lucía: Yes. We stripped off the newer paint and the 1798 colour came out.

Cliente: ¿El dorado queda impoluto?

Client: Will the gilding be spotless?

Lucía: Pulido a mano, como nuevo. Pulir lleva tiempo, pero la calidad lo pide.

Lucía: Hand-polished, like new. Polishing takes time, but quality demands it.

Cliente: El precio es justo. Hablamos el lunes.

Client: The price is fair. We'll talk on Monday.

New words in the dialogue: decapar (to strip), reintegración (infilling), impoluto (spotless) — all extension words from today's table.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Absolute participles: terminada la restauración…, una vez desmontado…, limpio ya el barniz… — participle agrees with its noun, no connector.
  • Infinitive as noun: restaurar es decidir, fumar es malo, el lijar y el pulir — masculine singular, never a gerund as subject.
  • Gerund clauses: lijando el marco…, encontró… — background action; habiéndose terminado… is advanced, read for recognition.

5.2. Further reading — el taller de cada día

Context: Everyday life in the workshop, after the altarpiece is gone. Read it quietly:

En el taller se restaura de todo: marcos, altares y frescos de pueblo.

In the workshop they restore everything: frames, altars and village frescoes.

El ebanista y el carpintero trabajan juntos desde hace años.

The cabinetmaker and the carpenter have worked together for years.

El aprendiz cataloga y documenta, meticuloso, cada mañana.

The apprentice catalogues and documents, meticulous, every morning.

A las dos se cierra y se habla del diagnóstico de la tarde.

At two they close up and discuss the afternoon's assessment.

Terminado el día, todos vuelven a casa contentos.

With the day finished, everyone goes home happy.

No new words here — everything comes from today's table. Notice the last line: another absolute participle (terminado el día).
