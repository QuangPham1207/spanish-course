LESSON 04 — FAMILY & PEOPLE

Lesson goals:
  • Talk about your family with padre, madre, hermano/a, hijo/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a.
  • Use possessive adjectives: mi, tu, su, nuestro (plural mis, tus, sus).
  • Say who you have with tener: Tengo dos hermanos, No tengo hijos.
  • Understand that su has several meanings (his, her, your, their).

Core vocabulary: familia, padre, madre, hermano/a, hijo/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a, mi/mis, tu/tus, su/sus, nuestro/a, con
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): persona, foto, grande, pequeño/a, mayor, menor, hijo único, diecinueve

1. READING PASSAGE (MY FAMILY)

Context: A student describes her family for a class. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Read top to bottom — the Spanish is enough to get the gist.)

Mi familia es grande. Somos seis personas.

My family is big. We are six people.

Mi padre se llama Pedro y mi madre se llama Rosa.

My father is called Pedro and my mother is called Rosa.

Tengo dos hermanos: Javier y Marta.

I have two siblings: Javier and Marta.

Mi hermano Javier tiene veinticinco años y es médico.

My brother Javier is twenty-five and he's a doctor.

Mi hermana Marta es estudiante; tiene diecinueve años.

My sister Marta is a student; she's nineteen.

También tengo un abuelo y una abuela.

I also have a grandfather and a grandmother.

Mis abuelos viven con nosotros.

My grandparents live with us.

Mis tíos y mis primos viven en Sevilla.

My uncles, aunts and cousins live in Seville.

Mi tío Carlos es el hermano de mi madre.

My uncle Carlos is my mother's brother.

¿Y tú? ¿Tienes hermanos?

And you? Do you have siblings?

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Possessive adjectives: mi, tu, su, nuestro

A possessive adjective says whose something is. In Spanish it agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner — so a woman says mi hermano (my brother), not mia hermano.

OwnerSingularPluralEnglish
yomimismy
tútutusyour (informal)
él / ella / ustedsusushis / her / your (formal)
nosotros / nosotrasnuestro / nuestranuestros / nuestrasour
vosotros / vosotras (Spain)vuestro / vuestravuestros / vuestrasyour (plural)
ellos / ellas / ustedessusustheir / your (plural)
  • mi hermano / mis hermanosmy brother / my brothers
  • tu madre / tus primosyour mother / your cousins
  • nuestra casa / nuestras hijasour house / our daughters
⚠️ tu vs tú: tu (no accent) = "your"; tú (with accent) = "you". ¿Tú tienes tu libro? = "Do you have your book?"
💡 su has four meanings: his, her, your (formal) and their. Context usually makes it clear. To be precise (advanced), Spanish can add de él / de ella / de usted / de ellos: su casa de ella = her house.
⚠️ No article with possessives: say mi madre, never la mi madre (this is a classic error for English and French speakers).

2.2. Talking about family with tener

tener (to have) is the verb for family members, just as for age. It is irregular only in the yo form (tengo).

tener + family member · ¿Tienes hermanos?
  • Tengo dos hermanos y una hermana.I have two brothers and one sister.
  • No tengo hijos.I don't have children.
  • ¿Tienes hermanos? — Sí, tengo uno.Do you have siblings? — Yes, I have one.
  • Soy hijo único. / Soy hija única.I'm an only child. (man / woman)
💡 Spain vs Latin America: "siblings" is hermanos in every variety; if you mean only brothers, say mis hermanos varones (advanced). The plural hermanos normally includes brothers and sisters.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences you can use from day one. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Describe your family:

Mi familia es grande. Somos seis personas.

My family is big. We are six people.

2. Say who you have:

Tengo dos hermanos y una hermana.

I have two brothers and one sister.

3. Ask about siblings:

¿Tienes hermanos? — No, soy hijo único.

Do you have siblings? — No, I'm an only child.

4. Introduce a parent:

Mi madre se llama Rosa.

My mother is called Rosa.

5. Say who lives with you:

Mis abuelos viven con nosotros.

My grandparents live with us.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends talk about their families in a café.

Pablo: ¿Tienes hermanos, Mei?

Pablo: Do you have siblings, Mei?

Mei: Sí, tengo un hermano y dos hermanas. ¿Y tú?

Mei: Yes, I have one brother and two sisters. And you?

Pablo: Yo soy hijo único, pero tengo muchos primos.

Pablo: I'm an only child, but I have many cousins.

Mei: ¿Dónde vive tu familia?

Mei: Where does your family live?

Pablo: Mi familia vive en México. Mis abuelos viven con nosotros.

Pablo: My family lives in Mexico. My grandparents live with us.

Mei: ¡Qué bien! Mi familia es pequeña: solo mi hermano y yo.

Mei: How nice! My family is small: just my brother and me.

Pablo: La familia es importante.

Pablo: Family is important.

New words in the dialogue: muchos (many), solo (only), importante (important).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Family words: familia, padre, madre, hermano/a, hijo/a, abuelo/a, tío/a, primo/a.
  • Possessives agree with the thing owned: mi, tu, su → plural mis, tus, sus; nuestro/a/os/as.
  • su can mean his, her, your (formal) or their — context decides.
  • Possession with tener: Tengo dos hermanos; No tengo hijos; Soy hijo único.
  • Never put an article before a possessive: mi madre, not la mi madre.

5.2. Further reading — the family in the Hispanic world

Context: A short note on family life. Read it quietly:

En el mundo hispano, la familia es muy importante.

In the Hispanic world, family is very important.

Muchas familias son grandes: abuelos, padres, hijos, tíos y primos.

Many families are large: grandparents, parents, children, uncles, aunts and cousins.

En muchos países, los abuelos viven con la familia.

In many countries, grandparents live with the family.

Una persona tiene dos apellidos: el primero del padre y el segundo de la madre.

A person has two surnames: the first from the father and the second from the mother.

Las familias hispanas celebran juntas los cumpleaños y las fiestas.

Hispanic families celebrate birthdays and holidays together.

New words: mundo (world), apellido (surname, last name), fiesta (party, holiday), celebrar (to celebrate).
