LESSON 87 — INTERVIEW: THE MAN WHO TUNES THE WOOD

Lesson goals:
  • Read an interview and profile, semi-glossed: direct speech and reported speech together.
  • Learn interview words: marimba, chonta, lutier, currulao.
  • Track the shift: dijo: "…" → dijo que… with tense backshift (L42).
  • Meet Néstor, an Afro-Colombian instrument maker from Guapi who teaches village kids.

Core vocabulary: marimba, chonta, lutier, tallar, afinar, bejuco, resonador, cantadora, currulao, arrullo, oficio, aprendiz, heredar, añadir, declarar, comillas, cita, mazo, ensayar, taller (plus known words madera, entrevista, pregunta, selva, río)
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): palma de chonta, calabaza, escenario, discurso referido

1. READING PASSAGE (EL HOMBRE QUE AFINA LA MADERA)

Context: In Guapi, on Colombia's Pacific coast, Néstor builds marimbas de chonta and teaches children to play. The interviewer's questions appear in direct speech; the profile retells his answers with dijo que, cuenta que, según. Semi-glossed: only the bold words are new.

(Bold words are in the vocabulary table. The English is only a check — read the Spanish first.)

GUAPI, CAUCA. En el taller de Néstor huele a madera mojada. Sobre dos bancos duerme una marimba a medio hacer: tablas oscuras de chonta y tubos de resonador atados con bejuco.

GUAPI, CAUCA. Néstor's workshop smells of wet wood. On two benches sleeps a half-made marimba: dark chonta boards and resonator tubes tied with vine.

—Maestro, ¿cuántos años lleva con este oficio? —pregunta la entrevistadora. Él sonríe: "Llevo treinta años. Este oficio lo heredé de mi padre".

"Maestro, how many years have you been in this craft?" asks the interviewer. He smiles: "I have been at it thirty years. I inherited this craft from my father."

Néstor cuenta que heredó el oficio de su padre y que lleva treinta años con él. Añade que cada marimba pide un árbol distinto y mucha paciencia.

Néstor says that he inherited the craft from his father and that he has been at it for thirty years. He adds that each marimba asks for a different tree and a lot of patience.

—"Yo no corto por cortar —declara—. Tallo la tabla, la dejo secar a la sombra y después la afino golpeándola con el mazo".

"I do not cut just to cut," he states. "I carve the board, I let it dry in the shade and then I tune it by striking it with the mallet."

Explica que no corta por cortar: dice que talla la tabla, que la deja secar a la sombra y que después la afina con el mazo. Sin las comillas, la cita respira igual.

He explains that he does not cut just to cut: he says that he carves the board, that he lets it dry in the shade and that he then tunes it with the mallet. Without the quotation marks, the quote breathes the same.

Por la tarde llegan los aprendices, doce niños del pueblo. Ensayan un currulao para la fiesta del río mientras una cantadora mayor les marca un arrullo con voz baja.

In the afternoon the apprentices arrive, twelve village kids. They rehearse a currulao for the river festival while an elder singer leads them in an arrullo in a low voice.

—"Si un niño aprende a afinar, aprende a escuchar" —dice Néstor. Según él, la marimba no es un objeto: es la memoria del pueblo.

"If a child learns to tune, he learns to listen," says Néstor. According to him, the marimba is not an object: it is the memory of the village.

Dijo que si un niño aprendía a afinar, aprendía a escuchar, y que la marimba no era un objeto sino la memoria del pueblo. El presente se hizo imperfecto al pasar a referido.

He said that if a child learned to tune, he learned to listen, and that the marimba was not an object but the memory of the village. The present became imperfect when shifted to reported speech.

Al despedirse, la entrevistadora escribe: Guapi suena a madera y a río. Y Néstor, con las manos llenas de aserrín, ya talla la próxima tabla.

As she says goodbye, the interviewer writes: Guapi sounds of wood and river. And Néstor, hands full of sawdust, is already carving the next board.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Direct vs reported speech: dijo "…" → dijo que…

Interviews live on this pair from Lesson 42. Direct speech keeps the original tense in quotes; reported speech adds que and shifts the tense back: present → imperfect, preterite → pluperfect, future → conditional.

  • "Llevo treinta años". → Cuenta que llevaba treinta años."I have been at it thirty years." → He says that he had been at it for thirty years.
  • "La marimba no es un objeto". → Dijo que la marimba no era un objeto."The marimba is not an object." → He said the marimba was not an object.
  • "Tallaré la próxima tabla". → Dijo que tallaría la próxima tabla."I will carve the next board." → He said he would carve the next board.

2.2. Interview markers: preguntar, añadir, declarar, según

Profiles rarely repeat decir. They preguntar (ask), añadir (add), declarar (state) — and they frame with según (according to). Each marker introduces a que-clause in reported speech.

  • —¿Cuántos años lleva? —pregunta la entrevistadora."How many years have you been at it?" asks the interviewer.
  • Añade que cada marimba pide un árbol distinto.He adds that each marimba asks for a different tree.
  • Según él, la marimba es la memoria del pueblo.According to him, the marimba is the memory of the village.
dijo: "…" → dijo que… + backshift (llevo → llevaba, es → era)

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences to read once. No need to memorize.

1. The craft:

Este oficio lo heredé de mi padre.

I inherited this craft from my father.

2. The wood:

Tallo la tabla y después la afino con el mazo.

I carve the board and then tune it with the mallet.

3. The kids:

Los aprendices ensayan un currulao para la fiesta.

The apprentices rehearse a currulao for the festival.

4. The voice:

La cantadora les marca un arrullo con voz baja.

The singer leads them in a lullaby in a low voice.

5. The memory:

Según él, la marimba es la memoria del pueblo.

According to him, the marimba is the memory of the village.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): read the five once. A whole workshop in five beats.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: The interviewer visits the workshop with a photographer. Just read.

Entrevistadora: Maestro Néstor, su taller huele a madera mojada y a río.

Interviewer: Maestro Néstor, your workshop smells of wet wood and river.

Néstor: Esta marimba de chonta es para mis aprendices del pueblo.

Néstor: This chonta marimba is for my village apprentices.

Entrevistadora: El currulao de ayer sonó muy limpio en cada resonador.

Interviewer: Yesterday's currulao sounded very clean in every resonator.

Néstor: La cantadora mayor ensayó el arrullo con ellos toda la tarde.

Néstor: The elder singer rehearsed the lullaby with them all afternoon.

Entrevistadora: Usted declara en su cita más famosa que afinar es escuchar.

Interviewer: You state in your most famous quote that tuning is listening.

Néstor: Añado hoy que tallar también es cuidar la memoria del oficio.

Néstor: I add today that carving is also caring for the memory of the craft.

New words in the dialogue: none — all from today's table or earlier lessons. Notice the interview's tools living in speech (marimba, chonta, resonador, cita) and the marker echo (declara… Añado).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Workshop words: marimba, chonta, lutier, mazo, taller — the furniture of Guapi.
  • Reported speech: dijo: "…" → dijo que…; llevo → llevaba; es → era (L42).
  • Markers: añade, declara, según — who frames the quote.

5.2. Further reading — la fiesta (semi-glossed)

Context: The profile closes at the river festival. Only two words are glossed — guess the rest:

En la fiesta del río, los aprendices tocan el currulao sin mirar las manos.

At the river festival, the apprentices play the currulao without looking at their hands.

La cantadora abre el arrullo y el pueblo entero responde con palmas.

The singer opens the lullaby and the whole village answers with clapping.

Néstor no sube al escenario: escucha desde su banco y afina de oído, feliz.

Néstor does not go up on stage: he listens from his bench and tunes by ear, happy.
