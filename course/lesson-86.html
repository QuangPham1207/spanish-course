LESSON 86 — HISTORICAL FEATURE: THE 1918 FLU

Lesson goals:
  • Read a historical feature, semi-glossed: dense paragraphs, dates and numbers, only feature words bolded.
  • Learn feature words: telegrama, oleada, mortandad, titular.
  • Read dates and years fluently: el 4 de octubre de 1918, mil novecientos dieciocho.
  • Meet Carmen, a night nurse in Zamora, and Dr. Fuentes, a port doctor in Veracruz.

Core vocabulary: telegrama, fallecer, neutralidad, pabellón, abarrotado, entierro, toser, remitir, provisional, tinta, fosa, aislar, desinfectar, muelle, tripulación, corresponsal, titular, trinchera, oleada, mortandad (plus known words gripe, enfermera, puerto, censura, cuarentena)
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): retaguardia, censura militar, barco correo, convalecencia

1. READING PASSAGE (LA GRIPE DE 1918: ZAMORA — VERACRUZ)

Context: Autumn 1918. A nurse in Zamora writes night notes while a port doctor in Veracruz waits for a ship from Spain. The flu was not born in Spain — but the world called it "Spanish". Semi-glossed: only the bold words are new.

(Bold words are in the vocabulary table. The English is only a check — read the Spanish first.)

ZAMORA, 4 DE OCTUBRE DE 1918. Terminada la primavera, el pabellón de infecciosos seguía abarrotado. Carmen, enfermera de noche, escribió con tinta espesa: hoy han fallecido siete personas.

ZAMORA, 4 OCTOBER 1918. With spring over, the infectious ward was still overcrowded. Carmen, a night nurse, wrote in thick ink: today seven people have died.

Los enfermos llegaban tosiendo sin parar; muchos no podían respirar y la fiebre no remitía en días. Se oía toser en los pasillos desde las tres de la mañana.

The sick arrived coughing without stop; many could not breathe and the fever did not subside for days. Coughing could be heard in the corridors from three in the morning.

En Madrid, la prensa hablaba ya de la epidemia con grandes titulares. España mantenía la neutralidad en la guerra, y sus periódicos publicaban lo que en Francia y Alemania callaba la censura.

In Madrid, the press already spoke of the epidemic with big headlines. Spain kept its neutrality in the war, and its newspapers published what censorship silenced in France and Germany.

Un corresponsal en París lo explicó así: en las trincheras morían miles de soldados, pero ningún diario podía escribirlo. Solo España, país neutral, contaba los muertos en portada.

A correspondent in Paris explained it like this: in the trenches thousands of soldiers were dying, but no newspaper could write it. Only Spain, a neutral country, counted the dead on its front pages.

VERACRUZ, 9 DE OCTUBRE DE 1918. Llegado el barco de La Habana, el doctor Fuentes subió al muelle con dos ayudantes. La tripulación presentaba fiebre alta y tos seca.

VERACRUZ, 9 OCTOBER 1918. With the ship from Havana arrived, Dr. Fuentes went up to the dock with two assistants. The crew showed high fever and dry cough.

El doctor dictó un telegrama urgente a la capital: aislar a los pasajeros, desinfectar el barco, preparar camas provisionales en el puerto. La segunda oleada ya estaba aquí.

The doctor dictated an urgent telegram to the capital: isolate the passengers, disinfect the ship, prepare makeshift beds in the port. The second wave was already here.

En Zamora, Carmen anotó el 22 de octubre de 1918: cuarenta y tres muertos en una semana. Faltaban ataúdes y los entierros se hacían de noche; se abrió una fosa común detrás del cementerio.

In Zamora, Carmen noted on 22 October 1918: forty-three dead in one week. Coffins ran out and burials were done at night; a mass grave was opened behind the cemetery.

Entre mayo de 1918 y abril de 1919, la mortandad en el mundo llegó a decenas de millones. Los historiadores hablan hoy de entre veinte y cincuenta millones de fallecidos.

Between May 1918 and April 1919, the death toll in the world reached tens of millions. Historians today speak of between twenty and fifty million dead.

Firmado el último parte de guardia, Carmen guardó el cuaderno. La gripe no era española; español era solo el nombre que le puso una prensa libre en un continente callado.

With the last duty report signed, Carmen put away the notebook. The flu was not Spanish; Spanish was only the name given to it by a free press on a silenced continent.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Dense dates and numbers: reading the timeline

Historical features are built on dates. Read years in full: 1918 = mil novecientos dieciocho. Day + month + year always takes el … de … de …, and periods take entre … y …. Order the tenses as in Lesson 33: pluperfect → preterite → present.

  • El 4 de octubre de 1918 (mil novecientos dieciocho) el pabellón seguía abarrotado.On 4 October 1918 the ward was still overcrowded.
  • Entre mayo de 1918 y abril de 1919 murieron decenas de millones.Between May 1918 and April 1919 tens of millions died.
  • Habían llegado tosiendo; el doctor dictó un telegrama; hoy la prensa lo cuenta así.They had arrived coughing; the doctor dictated a telegram; today the press tells it like this.

2.2. Absolute participles: Terminada la guerra…

You met these in Lesson 61. A participle + noun at the start of a sentence, with no haber, means "once / with X done". It agrees with the noun: Terminada la primavera, Llegado el barco, Firmado el parte.

  • Terminada la primavera, el pabellón seguía abarrotado.With spring over, the ward was still overcrowded.
  • Llegado el barco, el doctor subió al muelle.Once the ship had arrived, the doctor went up to the dock.
  • Firmado el parte, Carmen guardó el cuaderno.Once the report was signed, Carmen put away the notebook.
fecha (el 4 de octubre de 1918) + absoluto (Terminada…) → pretérito (dictó, abrió) → titular

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences to read once. No need to memorize.

1. The ward:

Terminada la primavera, el pabellón seguía abarrotado.

With spring over, the ward was still overcrowded.

2. The press:

España mantenía la neutralidad y publicaba grandes titulares.

Spain kept its neutrality and published big headlines.

3. The ship:

Llegado el barco, el doctor subió al muelle con dos ayudantes.

Once the ship arrived, the doctor went to the dock with two assistants.

4. The order:

Hay que aislar a los pasajeros y desinfectar el barco.

The passengers must be isolated and the ship disinfected.

5. The name:

La gripe no era española; español era solo el nombre.

The flu was not Spanish; Spanish was only the name.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): read the five once. A whole epidemic in five beats.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Carmen in Zamora speaks by telephone with Dr. Fuentes in Veracruz. They compare notes. Just read.

Carmen: Doctor, aquí en Zamora el pabellón sigue abarrotado esta noche.

Carmen: Doctor, here in Zamora the ward is still overcrowded tonight.

Fuentes: Lo mismo en el muelle. Acabo de dictar un telegrama a la capital.

Fuentes: The same at the dock. I have just dictated a telegram to the capital.

Carmen: La fiebre no remite y los entierros se hacen de noche.

Carmen: The fever does not subside and burials are done at night.

Fuentes: Hay que aislar a la tripulación y desinfectar cada camarote.

Fuentes: The crew must be isolated and every cabin disinfected.

Carmen: Terminada la guardia, escribo todo con tinta espesa en el cuaderno.

Carmen: Once the shift is over, I write everything in thick ink in the notebook.

Fuentes: Gracias, Carmen. Su parte de Zamora nos ayuda mucho en Veracruz.

Fuentes: Thank you, Carmen. Your report from Zamora helps us a lot in Veracruz.

New words in the dialogue: none — all from today's table or earlier lessons. Notice the feature's tools living in speech (pabellón, telegrama, aislar, desinfectar) and the absolute echo (Terminada la guardia).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Feature words: telegrama, pabellón, titular, oleada, mortandad — the furniture of 1918.
  • Dates: el 4 de octubre de 1918; entre mayo de 1918 y abril de 1919 — day, month, year.
  • Voice: Terminada la primavera… Llegado el barco… Firmado el parte… — absolute participles (L61).

5.2. Further reading — el nombre (semi-glossed)

Context: Why "Spanish"? A historian sums it up a century later. Only two words are glossed — guess the rest:

La gripe nació probablemente en cuarteles y puertos de medio mundo, no en España.

The flu was probably born in barracks and ports around the world, not in Spain.

Pero en 1918 solo la prensa de un país neutral podía contar la mortandad en grandes titulares.

But in 1918 only the press of a neutral country could report the death toll in big headlines.

El mundo leyó la noticia en español y, por pereza, la llamó española para siempre.

The world read the news in Spanish and, out of laziness, called it Spanish forever.

Almost unglossed — only mortandad and titular are marked (censorship, censura, you know since L49). This is the Ladder's promise: soon, no bold at all.
