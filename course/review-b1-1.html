REVIEW B1-1 — SHORT NEWS & OPINION PIECES

This is a re-reading lesson, not a test. There are no exercises and nothing to send. Read the two short texts and notice how much you already recognise.

What this review re-reads:
  • Lesson 33 — the pluperfect and the past-tense timeline.
  • Lesson 34 — relative clauses (que, quien, cuyo, lo que).
  • Lesson 35 — the present subjunctive: wishes and doubt.
  • Lesson 36 — the subjunctive with emotions and impersonal expressions.
  • Lesson 37 — the subjunctive in adverbial clauses.
  • Lesson 38 — the subjunctive in adjective clauses.

Almost every word here comes from Lessons 33-38. A few extra, very common words appear in context (listed after the texts). No need to memorise anything — re-reading is exactly how it settles in.

READING 1 — NEWS REPORT (UN PUEBLO QUE RENACE)

Context: A short news piece about a town after a fire. Read it from top to bottom — the English below is only a check.

Cuando los bomberos llegaron, el fuego ya había destruido varias casas.

When the firefighters arrived, the fire had already destroyed several houses.

Los vecinos, que habían perdido casi todo, se organizaron muy rápido.

The neighbours, who had lost almost everything, got organised very quickly.

Para que el pueblo se recupere, el gobierno prometió ayuda.

So that the town can recover, the government promised help.

Es importante que las familias reciban apoyo antes de que llegue el invierno.

It's important that the families receive support before winter arrives.

Aunque el daño fue grave, nadie resultó herido.

Although the damage was serious, nobody was injured.

Quienes viven allí dicen que no se rendirán.

Those who live there say they won't give up.

READING 2 — OPINION COLUMN (LA ESPERANZA DEL PUEBLO)

Context: An opinion piece about the same event. Read it quietly:

Me alegra que la comunidad haya reaccionado tan rápido.

I'm glad the community reacted so quickly.

Es una lástima que las autoridades hayan tardado tanto.

It's a shame that the authorities took so long.

Necesitamos un plan que proteja a los más pobres.

We need a plan that protects the poorest.

No creo que sea suficiente reconstruir las casas; hace falta trabajo.

I don't think rebuilding the houses is enough; jobs are needed.

Ojalá la ayuda llegue a todos, sin que nadie se quede atrás.

I hope the help reaches everyone, without anyone being left behind.

Lo que importa es que la gente tenga esperanza.

What matters is that the people have hope.

💡 Register note: a news report mainly uses facts (indicative, pluperfect) and a relative clause to add detail; an opinion column is where the subjunctive lives (emotion, doubt, wishes, purpose). You are now reading both.

THE SENTENCES, BROKEN INTO PATTERNS

Re-read these mini-groups. Each one is a pattern you can reuse forever.
