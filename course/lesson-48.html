LESSON 48 — INDEPENDENCE: BOLÍVAR, SAN MARTÍN, HIDALGO

Lesson goals:
  • Read about the Grito de Dolores (1810) and the Guayaquil meeting (1822).
  • Re-use the conditional (sería, tendría que) and si-clauses for history's what-ifs.
  • Re-use verb periphrases (acabar de, ponerse a, seguir + gerundio) to narrate how events unfolded.
  • Pick up words: independencia, criollo, libertador, proclama, caudillo.

Core vocabulary: independencia, criollo/a, peninsular, junta, libertador/a, proclama, manifiesto, levantamiento, sublevar, sublevación, caudillo, caudillismo, cabildo, realista, patriota, insurgente, montonera, derrota, victoria, liberar, constitución, constituyente, soberanía, congreso, diputado/a, nación, himno, campana, sermón, acta, prócer, heroína
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): parroquia, homenaje, efeméride

1. READING PASSAGE (DOS GRITOS Y UNA ENTREVISTA)

Context: Two scenes twelve years apart: a priest rings a bell in Mexico in 1810, and two liberators meet in Guayaquil in 1822. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Historians love what-ifs: the conditional imagines what would happen, and periphrases show how events unfolded.)

En 1810 la América española llevaba tres siglos obedeciendo a un rey lejano, pero los criollos — hijos de españoles nacidos en América — ya no aceptaban ser ciudadanos de segunda.

In 1810 Spanish America had been obeying a distant king for three centuries, but the criollos — children of Spaniards born in America — no longer accepted being second-class citizens.

Los peninsulares, nacidos en España, ocupaban los mejores cargos, y esa injusticia acababa de encender la primera sublevación cuando sonó una campana.

The peninsulares, born in Spain, held the best posts, and that injustice had just lit the first uprising when a bell rang.

El cura Hidalgo tocó la campana de su parroquia y lanzó un sermón que la historia recordaría como el Grito de Dolores: se puso a gritar ¡viva la Virgen y muera el mal gobierno!

The priest Hidalgo rang his parish bell and launched a sermon that history would remember as the Grito de Dolores: he started shouting "long live the Virgin and death to bad government!"

Los insurgentes siguieron marchando pueblo tras pueblo, y cada cabildo que tomaban firmaba un acta de independencia.

The insurgents kept marching town after town, and each town council they took signed an act of independence.

Si Hidalgo hubiera tenido artillería, dicen, habría tomado la capital; pero sin cañones su levantamiento fue aplastado y él fue fusilado.

If Hidalgo had had artillery, they say, he would have taken the capital; but without cannons his uprising was crushed and he was shot.

Doce años después, el continente seguía en guerra: el libertador Simón Bolívar bajaba de los Andes y el general San Martín acababa de liberar Lima.

Twelve years later, the continent was still at war: the liberator Simón Bolívar was coming down from the Andes and General San Martín had just liberated Lima.

Los dos se reunieron en Guayaquil, y lo que se dijeron nadie lo sabe: no hubo acta, no hubo testigos, solo una proclama fría al día siguiente.

The two met in Guayaquil, and what they said to each other nobody knows: there were no minutes, no witnesses, only a cold proclamation the next day.

San Martín se puso a preparar su barco esa misma noche; si se quedaba, tendría que compartir el mando, y él no quería otra guerra civil entre patriotas.

San Martín started preparing his ship that same night; if he stayed, he would have to share command, and he did not want another civil war among patriots.

Bolívar siguió luchando con sus montoneras de llaneros y pastores, y sus manifiestos prometían que la victoria sería de los pueblos libres.

Bolívar kept fighting with his montoneras of plainsmen and shepherds, and his manifestos promised that victory would belong to free peoples.

Tras la derrota realista de Ayacucho, los nuevos países volvieron a nacer: cada congreso escribió una constitución y eligió diputados.

After the royalist defeat at Ayacucho, the new countries were born again: each congress wrote a constitution and elected deputies.

La junta constituyente declaró que la soberanía residía en la nación, y mandó cantar un himno nuevo en cada plaza.

The constituent assembly declared that sovereignty resided in the nation, and ordered a new anthem to be sung in every square.

De aquellos años quedan próceres en bronce y una heroína poco recordada, Manuela Sáenz, que liberó a Bolívar de un atentado y sería olvidada un siglo.

From those years remain próceres in bronze and a little-remembered heroine, Manuela Sáenz, who saved Bolívar from an assassination attempt and would be forgotten for a century.

Sin los caudillos que siguieron, dicen los historiadores, la paz habría llegado antes; pero el caudillismo sería la enfermedad del siglo XIX.

Without the caudillos who followed, historians say, peace would have arrived earlier; but caudillismo would be the sickness of the 19th century.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The conditional & si-clauses (recycled from L39)

The conditional (-ía endings) imagines what would happen. In history it also reports the future seen from the past (sería olvidada = was to be forgotten).

si + imperfect subjunctive, + conditional  ·  tendría que + infinitive
  • Si se quedaba, tendría que compartir el mando.If he stayed, he would have to share command.
  • La victoria sería de los pueblos libres.Victory would belong to the free peoples.
  • Sin los caudillos, la paz habría llegado antes.Without the caudillos, peace would have arrived earlier. (conditional perfect, from L40)
⚠️ Common mistake: after si (if), never use the conditional: si se quedaba, not si se quedaría.

2.2. Periphrases: how events unfold (recycled from L44)

  • San Martín acababa de liberar Lima.San Martín had just liberated Lima. (just-done)
  • Se puso a preparar su barco esa misma noche.He started preparing his ship that same night. (sudden start)
  • Los insurgentes siguieron marchando pueblo tras pueblo.The insurgents kept marching town after town. (continuation)
  • Llevaba tres siglos obedeciendo. / Volvieron a nacer.It had been obeying for three centuries. / They were born again.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five conditional and periphrasis sentences. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Would have to:

Si se quedaba, tendría que compartir el mando.

If he stayed, he would have to share command.

2. Had just:

San Martín acababa de liberar Lima.

San Martín had just liberated Lima.

3. Started to:

Se puso a preparar su barco esa misma noche.

He started preparing his ship that same night.

4. Kept marching:

Los insurgentes siguieron marchando pueblo tras pueblo.

The insurgents kept marching town after town.

5. Would belong:

La victoria sería de los pueblos libres.

Victory would belong to the free peoples.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: A teacher and a student talk after class in Bogotá.

Alumna: Profe, ¿qué se dijeron Bolívar y San Martín en Guayaquil?

Student: Teacher, what did Bolívar and San Martín say to each other in Guayaquil?

Profe: Nadie lo sabe. No hubo acta, no hubo testigos.

Teacher: Nobody knows. There were no minutes, no witnesses.

Alumna: Si se quedaba San Martín, ¿habría dos libertadores?

Student: If San Martín had stayed, would there have been two liberators?

Profe: Tendría que compartir el mando, y él no quería otra guerra civil. Se fue.

Teacher: He would have had to share command, and he didn't want another civil war. He left.

Alumna: ¿Y Manuela Sáenz? Ella sí se quedó, ¿no?

Student: And Manuela Sáenz? She did stay, right?

Profe: Ella siguió luchando a su lado. Sin ella, Bolívar habría muerto en 1828.

Teacher: She kept fighting at his side. Without her, Bolívar would have died in 1828.

New words in the dialogue: mando (command), testigo (witness), atentado (assassination attempt).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • 1810 → 1822: Hidalgo's bell (campana, sermón), the levantamiento crushed; Bolívar and San Martín meet; constitución, soberanía, nación.
  • Conditional: sería, tendría que, habría llegado — results imagined from the past.
  • Periphrases: acabar de (just), ponerse a (start), seguir + gerundio (continue).
  • Word families: sublevar → sublevación; caudillo → caudillismo; libertador → liberar.

5.2. Further reading — la heroína olvidada

Context: Manuela Sáenz, the Quiteña who saved Bolívar. Read it quietly:

Manuela era criolla, leía a los filósofos franceses y se puso a conspirar cuando los realistas volvieron a Quito.

Manuela was a criolla, she read the French philosophers and started conspiring when the royalists returned to Quito.

En 1828 entró por la ventana del palacio, despertó a Bolívar y lo liberó de los asesinos; sin ella, la independencia habría perdido a su libertador.

In 1828 she climbed through the palace window, woke Bolívar and saved him from the assassins; without her, independence would have lost its liberator.

Los patriotas la llamaron la Libertadora, pero los generales la odiaban: una mujer con proclamas propias no cabía en su nación.

The patriots called her the Liberator, but the generals hated her: a woman with her own proclamations had no place in their nation.

Murió pobre en Paita, y su homenaje tardó un siglo; hoy cada 24 de mayo el Ecuador celebra su efeméride.

She died poor in Paita, and her tribute took a century; today every 24th of May Ecuador celebrates her anniversary.

Su parroquia guarda el acta de bautismo, y las niñas que la leen siguen preguntando por la heroína del balcón.

Her parish keeps the baptism record, and the girls who read it keep asking about the heroine of the balcony.

New words: balcón (balcony), conspirar (to conspire).
