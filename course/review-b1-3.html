REVIEW B1-3 — A HISTORY FEATURE

This is a re-reading lesson, not a test. There are no exercises and nothing to send. Read the two short texts and notice how much you already recognise.

What this review re-reads:
  • Lesson 45 — Al-Ándalus & medieval Spain (pluperfect timeline, relative clauses).
  • Lesson 46 — 1492: the fall of Granada, the expulsion, Columbus (passive ser/se, por/para).
  • Lesson 47 — Conquest & colonial Latin America (reported speech, lo que/quienes).
  • Lesson 48 — Independence: Bolívar, San Martín, Hidalgo (conditional, periphrases).

READING 1 — FEATURE (MIL AÑOS EN UNA CIUDAD)

Context: A magazine feature walks through Córdoba, Granada and Seville in a single day — a thousand years of history in three stops. Read it from top to bottom — the English below is only a check.

La mezquita que el emir había construido sigue en pie, y los traductores que tradujeron sus libros fueron recordados en cada universidad.

The mosque the emir had built still stands, and the translators who translated its books are remembered in every university.

Aquella convivencia de musulmanes y cristianos produjo un legado que ningún decreto pudo borrar.

That coexistence of Muslims and Christians produced a legacy that no decree could erase.

En 1492 Granada fue tomada por los reyes, y los judíos fueron expulsados para siempre de los puertos de Andalucía.

In 1492 Granada was taken by the monarchs, and the Jews were expelled forever from the ports of Andalusia.

El oro y la plata que llegaron después fueron contados en cofres, y los cronistas dijeron que la conquista había costado más de lo que valía.

The gold and silver that arrived afterwards were counted in chests, and the chroniclers said the conquest had cost more than it was worth.

Lo que queda de aquel mundo mestizo son palacios, templos y una herencia que América aún discute.

What remains of that mestizo world are palaces, temples and a heritage America still debates.

Los virreyes cobraban el tributo y firmaban tratados, mientras quienes trabajaban en la mina morían sin dejar testimonio.

The viceroys collected tribute and signed treaties, while those who worked in the mine died without leaving testimony.

READING 2 — FEATURE (DEL GRITO A LAS MADRES)

Context: The second half of the feature: from Hidalgo's bell to the mothers of the disappeared. Read it quietly:

El cura Hidalgo tocó la campana para que el pueblo despertara, y los insurgentes siguieron marchando pueblo tras pueblo.

The priest Hidalgo rang the bell so the people would wake, and the insurgents kept marching town after town.

Si los libertadores se hubieran puesto de acuerdo en Guayaquil, la independencia habría costado menos sangre.

If the liberators had agreed in Guayaquil, independence would have cost less blood.

Un siglo después, el golpe de 1936 dividió España en dos bandos, y el bombardeo de Guernica fue condenado en todo el mundo.

A century later, the 1936 coup divided Spain into two sides, and the bombing of Guernica was condemned worldwide.

Los exiliados escribían para que sus familias supieran que vivían, antes de que la censura cerrara cada carta.

The exiles wrote so their families would know they were alive, before censorship closed every letter.

Las dictaduras que siguieron fueron denunciadas por las madres de los desaparecidos, cuya marcha habría avergonzado a cualquier tribunal.

The dictatorships that followed were denounced by the mothers of the disappeared, whose march would have shamed any court.

Sin que nadie les pagara, los militantes reunían testimonios en cada asamblea, y cada aniversario se lee una biografía nueva.

Without anyone paying them, the militants gathered testimonies in every assembly, and each anniversary a new biography is read.

La memoria, dicen, es la única victoria segura: cada huelga, cada protesta y cada manifestación la mantienen viva.

Memory, they say, is the only certain victory: every strike, every protest and every demonstration keeps it alive.

💡 Register note: a history feature mixes every grammar you own: the pluperfect orders the centuries (había construido), the passive reports events (fue tomada), reported speech quotes the chronicles (dijeron que había costado), and the conditional imagines what-ifs (habría costado). You are now reading all of it at once.

THE SENTENCES, BROKEN INTO PATTERNS

Re-read these mini-groups. Each one is a pattern you can reuse forever.

Pluperfect & relatives (L45)

  • La mezquita que el emir había construido sigue en pie.The mosque the emir had built still stands.
  • Sabios cuyas obras se leerían durante siglos.Scholars whose works would be read for centuries.

Passive ser/se & por/para (L46)

  • Granada fue tomada por los reyes.Granada was taken by the monarchs.
  • Fueron expulsados para siempre de los puertos.They were expelled forever from the ports.
  • Se firmó el decreto en marzo.The decree was signed in March.

Reported speech & lo que/quienes (L47)

  • Dijeron que la conquista había costado más de lo que valía.They said the conquest had cost more than it was worth.
  • Lo que queda son palacios y una herencia mezclada.What remains are palaces and a mixed heritage.
  • Quienes trabajaban en la mina morían sin dejar testimonio.Those who worked in the mine died without leaving testimony.

Conditional & periphrases (L48)

  • La victoria sería de los pueblos libres.Victory would belong to the free peoples.
  • San Martín acababa de liberar Lima.San Martín had just liberated Lima.
  • Los insurgentes siguieron marchando.The insurgents kept marching.

Subjunctive adverbials (L49)

  • Tocó la campana para que el pueblo despertara.He rang the bell so the people would wake.
  • Escribían antes de que la censura cerrara cada carta.They wrote before censorship closed every letter.
  • Sin que nadie les pagara, reunían testimonios.Without anyone paying them, they gathered testimonies.

Conditional perfect & passive (L50)

  • La independencia habría costado menos sangre.Independence would have cost less blood.
  • Su marcha habría avergonzado a cualquier tribunal.Their march would have shamed any court.
  • Las dictaduras fueron denunciadas por las madres.The dictatorships were denounced by the mothers.
⚠️ One last reminder: the only thing a review asks is that you read. No writing, no exercises, no memorising. If a sentence felt easy today, that is progress.
