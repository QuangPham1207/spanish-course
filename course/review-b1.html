REVIEW B1 — A CULTURAL ESSAY (CLOSES B1)

This is a re-reading lesson, not a test. There are no exercises and nothing to send. Read the two short texts and notice how much you already recognise. This review closes the whole B1 level.

What this review re-reads:
  • Lesson 51 — art: Velázquez, Goya, Picasso, Kahlo, Rivera; the passive; cuyo.
  • Lesson 52 — Cervantes & Don Quijote; reported speech; the pluperfect.
  • Lesson 53 — the Latin American Boom; aunque; para que / antes de que; connectors.
  • Lesson 54 — cinema & television; si-clauses; periphrases.
  • Lesson 55 — music & contemporary culture; por / para; llevar / seguir + gerundio.

READING 1 — UN MUSEO Y UNA NOVELA

Context: The first half of a cultural essay: paintings and books that made history. Read it from top to bottom — the English below is only a check.

En la mejor sala del Prado hay un cuadro que mira a quien lo mira: dicen que es la obra maestra de Velázquez.

In the Prado's finest room there is a painting that looks back at whoever looks at it: they say it is Velázquez's masterpiece.

Fue pintado hace casi cuatro siglos, y todavía pregunta: ¿quién mira a quién?

It was painted almost four centuries ago, and it still asks: who is looking at whom?

En la sala de al lado, Goya, que quedó sordo a los cuarenta y seis años, llenó sus paredes de pesadillas.

In the next room, Goya, who went deaf at forty-six, filled his walls with nightmares.

Tres siglos después, Picasso pintó el Guernica contra el bombardeo, y Kahlo pintó su realidad en autorretratos pequeños.

Three centuries later Picasso painted Guernica against the bombing, and Kahlo painted her reality in small self-portraits.

Don Quijote salió de la imprenta en 1605 buscando ventura, y todavía no ha vuelto a casa.

Don Quijote came off the press in 1605 seeking fortune, and he still hasn't come home.

Cervantes dijo que solo contaba lo que escribió un historiador árabe, y los lectores le creyeron durante siglos.

Cervantes said he was only telling what an Arab historian wrote, and readers believed him for centuries.

Sancho gobernó su ínsula bien, y volvió pobre y contento.

Sancho governed his isle well, and returned poor and happy.

READING 2 — PANTALLAS, CANCIONES Y PALABRAS

Context: The second half: films, music and the language itself. Read it quietly:

Cuarón rodó Roma en su barrio de niño, con una protagonista que trabajaba como maestra.

Cuarón shot Roma in his childhood neighbourhood, with a lead actress who worked as a teacher.

La proyección es en blanco y negro y hablan mixteco: el cine también es lengua.

The screening is in black and white and they speak Mixtec: cinema is language too.

Del Toro mezcla terror y fantasía, y Almodóvar pone a las mujeres en el centro de sus melodramas.

Del Toro mixes horror and fantasy, and Almodóvar puts women at the centre of his melodramas.

En la discoteca bailan dembow y todos tararean el estribillo, aunque nadie sabe inglés.

In the club they dance to dembow and everyone hums the chorus, although nobody knows English.

Rosalía canta con duende y Bad Bunny hace colaboraciones con todos: la colaboración es la norma de hoy.

Rosalía sings with duende and Bad Bunny collaborates with everyone: collaboration is today's norm.

El lunfardo juega con el doble sentido, y el tango lo canta.

Lunfardo plays with double meanings, and tango sings it.

Y la lengua sigue viva: del voseo de Buenos Aires al espanglish de Miami, pasando por el seseo de todos.

And the language stays alive: from Buenos Aires voseo to Miami Spanglish, via everyone's seseo.

💡 Register note: an essay mixes every grammar you know: the passive for art (fue pintado), reported speech for books (dijo que), aunque for contrasts, si for conditions, por/para for reasons — and the subjunctive whenever someone wants, doubts or feels. You are now reading all of it.

THE SENTENCES, BROKEN INTO PATTERNS

Re-read these mini-groups. Each one is a pattern you can reuse forever.

The passive and relatives (L51)

  • Fue pintado hace casi cuatro siglos.It was painted almost four centuries ago.
  • Goya, que quedó sordo, llenó sus paredes de pesadillas.Goya, who went deaf, filled his walls with nightmares.
  • El tango, cuyas palabras vienen del lunfardo, nació en el puerto.Tango, whose words come from lunfardo, was born in the port.

Reported speech and the pluperfect (L52)

  • Dijo que solo contaba lo que escribió un historiador árabe.He said he was only telling what an Arab historian wrote.
  • Cuando salió la parte falsa, Cervantes ya había escrito la verdadera.When the false part came out, Cervantes had already written the true one.

Concessions and purpose (L53)

  • Aunque nadie sabe inglés, todos tararean el estribillo.Although nobody knows English, everyone hums the chorus.
  • Hay dos caminos para que el lector elija.There are two paths for the reader to choose from.
  • Lo imposible es normal; es decir, Macondo.The impossible is normal; that is, Macondo.

Conditions and periphrases (L54)

  • Si la ves en versión original, lees los subtítulos.If you watch it in the original version, you read the subtitles.
  • Si ganara el Óscar, hablaría de su madre.If he won the Oscar, he would talk about his mother.
  • Y la lengua sigue viva.And the language stays alive.

Cause, purpose and duration (L55)

  • Toca por dinero, pero canta para su familia.He plays for money, but he sings for his family.
  • Lleva veinte años cantando.He has been singing for twenty years.

Wishes and relatives (L56)

  • Es importante que el quechua viva en la escuela.It's important for Quechua to live on at school.
  • Quienes hablan guaraní viven en dos mundos.Those who speak Guaraní live in two worlds.
  • Lo que más me gusta es la sobremesa.What I like most is the after-meal chat.
⚠️ One last reminder: the only thing a review asks is that you read. No writing, no exercises, no memorising. If a sentence felt easy today, that is progress.
