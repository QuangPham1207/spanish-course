LESSON 50 — 20TH-CENTURY LATIN AMERICA: REVOLUTIONS & DICTATORSHIPS

Lesson goals:
  • Read about rigged elections, guerrillas, the disappeared — and the mothers who marched.
  • Re-use the conditional perfect (habría ganado) for results that never happened.
  • Re-use the passive with ser and se for arrests, trials and headlines.
  • Pick up words: dictadura, guerrilla, desaparecido, fraude, manifestación.

Core vocabulary: dictadura, guerrilla, militante, clandestino/a, desaparición, desaparecido/a, tortura, golpista, fraude, elecciones, votación, presidente/a, neoliberal, madres, testimonio, aniversario, biografía, autonomía, campesino/a, obrero/a, reforma, federal, desierto, istmo, murga, manifestación, protesta, marcha, paro, asamblea, embajada, asilo, tribunal, cadena
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): micrófono, radio, estadio

1. READING PASSAGE (LAS MADRES DE LA PLAZA)

Context: From Mexico 1968 to Buenos Aires 1977: how dictatorships fell and how mothers, singers and voters resisted. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(The conditional perfect imagines what would have happened; the passive reports what was done.)

En 1968, los estudiantes mexicanos convocaron una manifestación pacífica en Tlatelolco; la protesta fue reprimida con una matanza que el gobierno ocultó durante décadas.

In 1968, Mexican students called a peaceful demonstration in Tlatelolco; the protest was crushed with a massacre the government hid for decades.

El presidente dijo después que no sabía nada, pero los testimonios reunidos habrían bastado para abrir un juicio.

The president later said he knew nothing, but the gathered testimonies would have sufficed to open a trial.

Sin aquellas balas, dicen los historiadores, el país habría abierto su sistema mucho antes; en cambio, el partido único siguió ganando cada votación.

Without those bullets, historians say, the country would have opened its system much earlier; instead, the single party kept winning every vote.

Las elecciones se celebraban cada seis años, pero el fraude era tan abierto que los militantes decían que el presidente ya estaba elegido antes de que votara nadie.

Elections were held every six years, but fraud was so open that militants said the president was already chosen before anyone voted.

En el campo, los campesinos pedían la reforma agraria y los obreros organizaban el paro general; la respuesta del gobierno fue detener a los líderes.

In the countryside, peasants demanded agrarian reform and workers organized the general strike; the government's answer was to arrest the leaders.

En Chile, el cantante Víctor Jara cantaba en estadios y fábricas con su guitarra; tras el golpe de 1973 fue detenido y asesinado en un estadio.

In Chile, the singer Víctor Jara sang in stadiums and factories with his guitar; after the 1973 coup he was arrested and murdered in a stadium.

Si su voz no hubiera cruzado las fronteras, el mundo habría tardado más en saberlo; sus canciones fueron prohibidas, pero cada cadena de radio extranjera las repetía.

If his voice had not crossed borders, the world would have taken longer to learn of it; his songs were banned, but every foreign radio network repeated them.

En Argentina, la dictadura de 1976 instaló centros clandestinos donde la tortura era rutina y la desaparición era el método.

In Argentina, the 1976 dictatorship set up clandestine centres where torture was routine and disappearance was the method.

Los golpistas decían que combatían a la guerrilla, pero fueron secuestrados miles de jóvenes que jamás habían tomado un arma.

The coup-makers said they were fighting the guerrilla, but thousands of young people who had never taken up a weapon were kidnapped.

Cada jueves, las madres de los desaparecidos empezaron a dar vueltas a la plaza con pañuelos blancos: su marcha silenciosa habría avergonzado a cualquier tribunal.

Every Thursday, the mothers of the disappeared began circling the square with white headscarves: their silent march would have shamed any court.

Pedían asilo en cada embajada y reunían testimonios en cada asamblea barrial, para que el mundo supiera los nombres.

They sought asylum in every embassy and gathered testimonies in every neighbourhood assembly, so the world would know the names.

En el desierto de Atacama y en el istmo centroamericano quedaron fosas que las comisiones de la verdad abrirían décadas después.

In the Atacama desert and on the Central American isthmus remained graves that the truth commissions would open decades later.

Con la vuelta de la democracia, cada aniversario se lee una biografía distinta; los federales discuten con las provincias que piden autonomía.

With the return of democracy, each anniversary a different biography is read; the federal government argues with provinces demanding autonomy.

Y en cada carnaval, la murga canta lo que los diarios callaron: sin memoria, dice, la dictadura volvería.

And at each carnival, the murga sings what the dailies kept silent: without memory, it says, dictatorship would return.

💡 Notice: habría abierto, habría tardado, habrían tomado — the conditional perfect (habría + participle) imagines a past that never happened. fue reprimida, fueron prohibidas, fueron secuestrados — the ser passive, agreeing in gender and number.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The conditional perfect (recycled from L40)

Habría + participle says what would have happened. History uses it for every turning point that went the other way.

habría + participle (would have …)
  • El país habría abierto su sistema mucho antes.The country would have opened its system much earlier.
  • El mundo habría tardado más en saberlo.The world would have taken longer to learn of it.
  • Su marcha habría avergonzado a cualquier tribunal.Their march would have shamed any court.

2.2. Passive ser and se for arrests and headlines (recycled from L41)

  • La protesta fue reprimida con una matanza.The protest was crushed with a massacre. (feminine singular agrees)
  • Sus canciones fueron prohibidas.His songs were banned. (feminine plural agrees)
  • Las elecciones se celebraban cada seis años.Elections were held every six years. (se-passive, agent hidden)
  • Fueron secuestrados miles de jóvenes.Thousands of young people were kidnapped.
⚠️ Common mistake: the participle agrees with the subject: fueron prohibidas (canciones), fueron secuestrados (jóvenes). Never leave it in the masculine singular by default.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five conditional-perfect and passive sentences. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Would have opened:

El país habría abierto su sistema mucho antes.

The country would have opened its system much earlier.

2. Was crushed:

La protesta fue reprimida con una matanza.

The protest was crushed with a massacre.

3. Were banned:

Sus canciones fueron prohibidas.

His songs were banned.

4. Would have shamed:

Su marcha habría avergonzado a cualquier tribunal.

Their march would have shamed any court.

5. Were held:

Las elecciones se celebraban cada seis años.

Elections were held every six years.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: A mother and daughter visit the former ESMA memorial in Buenos Aires.

Hija: Mamá, ¿quiénes son las mujeres del pañuelo blanco?

Daughter: Mum, who are the women with the white headscarves?

Madre: Las madres de los desaparecidos. Cada jueves marchaban en la plaza.

Mother: The mothers of the disappeared. Every Thursday they marched in the square.

Hija: ¿Y qué pedían?

Daughter: And what did they ask for?

Madre: Que les dijeran dónde estaban sus hijos. Testimonios, nombres, justicia.

Mother: To be told where their children were. Testimonies, names, justice.

Hija: Si no hubieran marchado, ¿nadie habría sabido nada?

Daughter: If they hadn't marched, would nobody have known anything?

Madre: Exacto. Sin su marcha, el mundo habría tardado mucho más.

Mother: Exactly. Without their march, the world would have taken much longer.

New words in the dialogue: pañuelo (headscarf), justicia (justice), memorial (memorial).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • 1968 → today: manifestación, fraude, guerrilla, dictadura → desaparecidos, tortura → madres, testimonios, asambleas.
  • Conditional perfect: habría abierto, habría tardado = what would have happened.
  • Passive agreement: fue reprimida, fueron prohibidas, fueron secuestrados.
  • Spain-vs-LatAm: paro = strike here; in Spain el paro usually means unemployment.

5.2. Further reading — la guitarra de Víctor

Context: Víctor Jara, singer of the Chilean New Song, murdered in 1973. Read it quietly:

Jara cantaba para campesinos y obreros que jamás habían entrado en un teatro.

Jara sang for peasants and workers who had never entered a theatre.

Sus canciones fueron grabadas en cintas baratas que las cadenas extranjeras repetían cada aniversario.

His songs were recorded on cheap tapes that foreign networks repeated every anniversary.

Sin su guitarra, la protesta habría perdido su voz más dulce; con ella, la marcha tenía música.

Without his guitar, the protest would have lost its sweetest voice; with it, the march had music.

Hoy su biografía se lee en las escuelas, y cada murga joven canta su nombre ante el micrófono.

Today his biography is read in schools, and every young murga sings his name into the microphone.

En el estadio que lleva su nombre, la radio local transmite el homenaje y nadie pide asilo: solo justicia.

In the stadium that bears his name, local radio broadcasts the tribute and nobody asks for asylum: only justice.

New words: cinta (tape), transmitir (to broadcast).
