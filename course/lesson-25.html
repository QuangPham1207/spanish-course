LESSON 25 — OBJECT PRONOUNS & GUSTAR-TYPE VERBS

Lesson goals:
  • Replace a thing with a direct object pronoun: lo, la, los, las — ¿Las entradas? Las compro yo.
  • Show who receives something with an indirect object pronoun: me, te, le, nos, os, les.
  • Master gustar-type verbs: doler, faltar, sobrar, interesar, importar, molestar.
  • Combine them: te lo doy, me la presta, se lo dije.

Core vocabulary: lo, la, los, las, me, te, le, nos, os, les, molestar, importar, faltar, sobrar, interesar, dar, decir, ver, ayudar, prestar, devolver, regalar, mirar, ópera, flamenco, pasión, músico/música
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): máquina, escenario, aplaudir

1. READING PASSAGE (UNA CONVERSACIÓN SOBRE MÚSICA)

Context: Two friends talk about music and tickets. The pronouns are in bold. Read each line; the English below is only a check.

(Notice how often a small word like lo or te replaces a whole noun.)

— ¿Te gusta el café? — Sí, me encanta, pero me molesta el ruido de las máquinas.

— Do you like coffee? — Yes, I love it, but the noise of the machines bothers me.

— A mí también me interesa el té; lo tomo todas las mañanas.

— Tea interests me too; I drink it every morning.

— ¿Conoces a Marta? — Sí, la conozco; le escribo a menudo.

— Do you know Marta? — Yes, I know her; I write to her often.

— Ella tiene dos entradas para el concierto. ¿Las quieres?

— She has two tickets for the concert. Do you want them?

— ¡Claro! Las compro yo. ¿Y a ti te faltan entradas?

— Of course! I'll buy them. And are you short of tickets?

— No, a mí me sobra una; te la puedo dar.

— No, I have one too many; I can give it to you.

— ¡Qué bien! Me importa mucho la música.

— How nice! Music matters a lot to me.

— A nosotros nos falta tiempo, pero nos gusta mucho la ópera.

— We don't have enough time, but we really like opera.

— Yo prefiero el flamenco; me parece increíble.

— I prefer flamenco; it seems incredible to me.

— Los músicos lo tocan con mucha pasión.

— The musicians play it with great passion.

💡 The big idea: in me molesta el ruido, the noise is really the subject and me means "to me". These verbs flip the sentence around.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Direct object pronouns: lo, la, los, las

The direct object is the thing you verb. Replace it with a pronoun so you don't repeat the noun. The pronoun agrees in gender and number.

PersonPronounExample
masculine singularlo¿El té? Lo tomo.
feminine singularla¿La entrada? La compro.
masculine plurallos¿Los libros? Los leo.
feminine plurallas¿Las entradas? Las compro.
  • ¿Ves la casa? — Sí, la veo.Can you see the house? — Yes, I see it.
  • El periódico no lo leo nunca.I never read the newspaper.
  • ¿Conoces a Marta? — Sí, la conozco.Do you know Marta? — Yes, I know her.
⚠️ With a person, Spanish adds the "personal a" in the full sentence: Conozco a Marta. The pronoun stays la.

2.2. Indirect object pronouns: me, te, le, nos, os, les

The indirect object is the person who receives something (the "to whom / for whom"). It works for men and women, singular and plural alike.

PersonPronounExample
a mímeMe da el libro.
a titeTe presto mi coche.
a él / a ella / a ustedleLe escribo a Marta.
a nosotrosnosNos ayuda mucho.
a vosotrososOs llamo luego.
a ellos / a ellas / a ustedeslesLes doy las entradas.
  • Te presto el bolígrafo.I'll lend you the pen.
  • Le devuelvo el libro a Juan.I return the book to Juan.
  • Nos regalan flores.They give us flowers as a gift.
  • ¿Me ayudas, por favor?Can you help me, please?

2.3. Two pronouns together: me lo, te la, se lo

When a direct and an indirect pronoun meet, the indirect comes first. le / les change to se before lo, la, los, las.

indirect + direct: me lo · te la · nos los · se lo · se las
  • ¿Me das el lápiz? — Sí, te lo doy.Can you give me the pencil? — Yes, I'll give it to you.
  • Te la puedo dar.I can give it to you. (la = la entrada)
  • Se lo dije ayer.I told it to him yesterday.
  • No se las compro.I won't buy them for her.
💡 Placement: pronouns go before a conjugated verb (lo tomo), but may attach to an infinitive or affirmative command (quiero tomarlo / tómalo).

2.4. gustar-type verbs

These verbs use the indirect pronouns and a thing as subject. Use singular when the thing is singular and plural when it is plural.

VerbMeaningExample
gustarto like (be pleasing)Me gusta el té. / Me gustan los libros.
encantarto loveMe encanta la música.
interesarto interestMe interesa la historia.
importarto matterMe importa la familia.
molestarto botherMe molesta el ruido.
dolerto hurtMe duele la cabeza.
faltarto be missing, to lackMe faltan dos euros.
sobrarto have too manyMe sobra tiempo.
  • A nosotros nos falta tiempo.We don't have enough time.
  • A mí me sobra una entrada.I have one ticket too many.
  • ¿Te molesta la música?Does the music bother you?
  • No me importa esperar.I don't mind waiting.
💡 Spain vs Latin America: both use these verbs the same way. For emphasis or clarity, Spanish adds a mí, a ti, a ella… at the start: A mí me gusta más el flamenco.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences with pronouns. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Replace a thing:

¿Tienes las llaves? — Sí, las tengo.

Do you have the keys? — Yes, I have them.

2. Say who receives:

Le devuelvo el libro a mi hermana.

I return the book to my sister.

3. Join two pronouns:

¿Me puedes prestar el coche? — Sí, te lo presto.

Can you lend me the car? — Yes, I'll lend it to you.

4. Use a gustar-type verb:

A mí me interesa el arte; me faltan horas en el día.

Art interests me; I don't have enough hours in the day.

5. Say what doesn't matter:

No me importa el ruido; me gusta la música fuerte.

The noise doesn't bother me; I like loud music.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: A friend helps another with a small problem.

Sara: ¿Puedes ayudarme un momento? Me falta una entrada.

Sara: Can you help me for a moment? I'm short of one ticket.

Iván: Claro. A mí me sobra una. Te la doy.

Iván: Sure. I have one too many. I'll give it to you.

Sara: ¡Gracias! ¿La necesitas tú?

Sara: Thanks! Do you need it yourself?

Iván: No, ya la vi. Además, no me interesa mucho ese grupo.

Iván: No, I've already seen it. Besides, that band doesn't interest me much.

Sara: ¿De verdad? A mí me encanta. Los escucho todos los días.

Sara: Really? I love them. I listen to them every day.

Iván: Pues entonces te la regalo. ¿Me invitás a otra cosa?

Iván: Then I'll give it to you. Will you treat me to something else?

Sara: ¡Hecho! Te invito a cenar después del concierto.

Sara: Deal! I'll treat you to dinner after the concert.

New words in the dialogue: ya (already — Lesson 20), grupo (band, group), invitar (to invite, to treat), hecho (done, deal), otra cosa (something else).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Direct: lo, la, los, las replace the thing — las entradas → las compro.
  • Indirect: me, te, le, nos, os, les mark the receiver — le escribo a Marta.
  • Together: indirect then direct, and le/les → se: te lo doy, se lo dije.
  • gustar-type: gustar, encantar, doler, faltar, sobrar, interesar, importar, molestar use me/te/le….
  • Singular vs plural: me gusta el té / me gustan los libros.

5.2. Further reading — a message about a concert

Context: A short message between friends. Read it quietly:

¡Hola! ¿Te apetece ir al concierto del sábado?

Hi! Do you feel like going to Saturday's concert?

Tengo dos entradas y no me costaron nada: me las regaló mi hermano.

I have two tickets and they didn't cost me anything: my brother gave them to me.

La música me interesa mucho, pero a ti te gusta más el flamenco.

The music interests me a lot, but you like flamenco more.

Si te apetece, te la doy sin problema; a mí me sobra una.

If you feel like it, I'll give it to you, no problem; I have one too many.

Dime algo pronto, por favor. ¡Me encantaría ir contigo!

Tell me something soon, please. I'd love to go with you!

New words: apetecer (to feel like), costar (to cost — Lesson 09), contigo (with you), cualquier cosa (anything).
