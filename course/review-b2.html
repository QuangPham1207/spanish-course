REVIEW B2 / FINAL — RE-READ THE CAPSTONE

This is a re-reading lesson, not a test. There are no exercises and nothing to send. Read the two short texts and notice how much of the Reading Ladder you already recognise — then keep reading for life.

What this review re-reads:
  • Lesson 81 — reading strategies (contexto, en diagonal, de un vistazo…).
  • Lesson 82 — news report (incendio, controlado, según fuentes…).
  • Lesson 83 — opinion column (postura, a mi juicio, convivir…).
  • Lesson 84 — cultural essay (velada, comensal, demora…).
  • Lesson 85 — short story (desván, aldaba, umbral…).
  • Lesson 86 — historical feature: dense narrative and dates.
  • Lesson 87 — interview and profile: direct speech with reported speech.
  • Lesson 88 — formal and academic writing: abstract, report, official letter.
  • Lesson 89 — full authentic article, dictionary only: all strategies together.
  • Lesson 90 — capstone story (bibliotecaria, donación, convoy…).

Almost every word here comes from Lessons 81-90. A few extra, very common words appear in context (listed after the texts). No need to memorise anything — re-reading is exactly how it settles in.

READING 1 — THE TOOLS, THE FIRE AND THE TABLE (L81–L84)

Context: One page that walks the first half of the Ladder: how to read, a fire bulletin, a columnist's stance, and a long evening at the table. Read it from top to bottom — the English below is only a check.

Lee primero en diagonal y adivina por el contexto: de un vistazo ya sabes si el texto es noticia o columna.

Read in diagonal first and guess from context: at a glance you already know whether the text is news or column.

El parte dice que el incendio ya está controlado: los bomberos desalojaron dos casas y el foco principal se estabilizó.

The bulletin says the fire is already under control: firefighters evacuated two houses and the main front stabilised.

Según fuentes del operativo, el perímetro aguanta y solo hay daños materiales.

According to operation sources, the perimeter is holding and there is only material damage.

A mi juicio, dice la columna, es evidente que la ciudad necesita otra postura: convivir, ceder el paso, compartir la calzada.

In my judgment, says the column, it is evident the city needs another stance: coexisting, yielding, sharing the roadway.

Cabe preguntarse con qué matiz responde la réplica del martes.

One may ask with what nuance Tuesday's reply responds.

Por la noche, la velada de los comensales: el anfitrión propone el brindis y la charla amena sigue.

At night, the diners' evening: the host proposes the toast and the pleasant chat goes on.

La demora se vuelve deleite; sobre el mantel solo quedan las migajas y una carcajada.

Lingering turns to delight; on the tablecloth only crumbs and a burst of laughter remain.

READING 2 — THE HOUSE AND THE TRAINS (L85–L90)

Context: A second text, on the other half of the Ladder — Alba's house, her library, and the trains that feed it. Read it quietly:

En la penumbra del desván, el baúl seguía esperando, y la mecedora crujía junto a la ventana.

In the half-light of the attic, the trunk kept waiting, and the rocking chair creaked by the window.

Alba susurró su nombre en el umbral, y la aldaba golpeó dos veces, o eso creyó ella.

Alba whispered her name on the threshold, and the knocker struck twice — or so she believed.

Antaño blanca, la casa es ahora biblioteca: la bibliotecaria abre cada mañana y espera el convoy bajo la marquesina.

Once white, the house is now a library: the librarian opens every morning and waits for the train under the canopy.

Las donaciones llegan en el tren: tomos con dedicatoria y fichas del catálogo, para otra estantería.

Donations arrive by train: volumes with dedications and catalogue cards, for another bookshelf.

El timbre suena tres veces y las campanadas contestan: cada lector recibe su carnet y cada libro su vencimiento.

The bell rings three times and the clock strokes answer: each reader receives a card and each book a due date.

La encuadernación más seria, el traqueteo, el silbido, el hollín: la consigna tiene los paquetes del pueblo.

The most solemn binding, the clatter, the whistle, the soot: the left-luggage holds the village's parcels.

Y este es el epílogo: la casa ya no espera trenes perdidos, los encuentra.

And this is the epilogue: the house no longer waits for lost trains, it finds them.

💡 Ladder note: a capstone reader mixes everything at once — strategies (en diagonal, de un vistazo), news (fue controlado, se estabilizó), stance (a mi juicio, es evidente que), essay (la demora, el deleite) and literary voice (o eso creyó ella). You are now reading all five at once.

THE SENTENCES, BROKEN INTO PATTERNS

Re-read these mini-groups. Each one is a pattern you can reuse forever.

Strategies, from L81 to L89

  • Lee en diagonal y adivina por el contexto.Skim and guess from context.
  • Relee y comprueba en el diccionario.Re-read and check in the dictionary.

News bulletins (L82)

  • El incendio fue controlado, casa por casa.The fire was brought under control, house by house.
  • Según fuentes, el perímetro aguanta.According to sources, the perimeter is holding.

Stance markers (L83)

  • A mi juicio, es evidente que la ciudad necesita otra postura.In my judgment, it is evident the city needs another stance.
  • No es que sea enemigo, sino que el diseño obliga.It's not that he's the enemy, but that design forces him.

Essay rituals (L84)

  • La demora no es pereza.Lingering is not laziness.
  • Cada comensal trae su historia.Every diner brings a story.

Literary pasts (L85)

  • La casa seguía en la colina. Alba empujó la puerta.The house still stood on the hill. Alba pushed the door.
  • La mecedora dormía. La aldaba golpeó dos veces.The chair slept. The knocker struck twice.

Dates and other voices (L86–L87)

  • En 1968 el tren dejó de pasar; en 2026 volvió.In 1968 the train stopped running; in 2026 it returned.
  • Dice que vendrá mañana; dijo que venía.She says she'll come tomorrow; she said she was coming.

Formal paper (L88)

  • La biblioteca fue inaugurada a las cinco.The library was inaugurated at five.
  • La carta fue leída en voz alta.The letter was read aloud.

Capstone voice (L90)

  • Que entre gente, que salgan libros.Let people come in, let books go out.
  • Ya no espera trenes perdidos: los encuentra.It no longer waits for lost trains: it finds them.
⚠️ One last reminder: the only thing a review asks is that you read. No writing, no exercises, no memorising. If a sentence felt easy today, that is progress — and watch the voices: scenes take the imperfect (llegaba, esperaba), events the preterite (sonó, abrió), stances their markers (a mi juicio, es evidente que).
