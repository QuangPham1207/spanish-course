LESSON 28 — FOOD CULTURE OF THE HISPANIC WORLD

Lesson goals:
  • Read about the great dishes of the Spanish-speaking world: paella, tacos, arepas, asado.
  • Learn the food vocabulary of each region.
  • See how a country's history and geography shape what it eats.
  • Meet the everyday impersonal se (advanced, read for exposure).

Core vocabulary: variado/a, región, plato, arroz, marisco, pollo, verdura, tortilla, maíz, cilantro, salsa, picante, arepa, queso, huevo, asado, parrilla, tradición, bebida, mate, chocolate, alimento, reunir, compartir
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): popular, redondo/a, Venezuela, Uruguay

1. READING PASSAGE (LA COMIDA DEL MUNDO HISPANO)

Context: A short article about food in the Spanish-speaking world. Read each line; the English below is only a check.

(Food is one of the easiest ways to see how different — and how connected — these countries are.)

La comida del mundo hispano es muy variada, porque cada región tiene sus platos.

The food of the Spanish-speaking world is very varied, because each region has its own dishes.

En España, el plato más famoso es la paella: un arroz con mariscos, pollo o verduras.

In Spain, the most famous dish is paella: a rice dish with seafood, chicken or vegetables.

Se prepara en una sartén grande y se comparte con la familia.

It is prepared in a large pan and shared with the family.

En México encontramos los tacos: tortillas de maíz con carne, cebolla, cilantro y salsa.

In Mexico we find tacos: corn tortillas with meat, onion, coriander and sauce.

La salsa puede ser muy picante.

The sauce can be very spicy.

En Venezuela y Colombia, las arepas son muy populares: panes redondos de maíz que se comen con queso, carne o huevo.

In Venezuela and Colombia, arepas are very popular: round corn breads eaten with cheese, meat or egg.

En Argentina y Uruguay, el asado es una gran tradición: carne a la parrilla los fines de semana.

In Argentina and Uruguay, the asado is a great tradition: grilled meat at the weekends.

Cada país tiene también sus bebidas: el mate en el sur, el café en Colombia, el chocolate en México.

Each country also has its drinks: mate in the south, coffee in Colombia, chocolate in Mexico.

La comida no es solo alimento: es una forma de reunir a la familia y de celebrar.

Food is not only nourishment: it's a way of bringing the family together and celebrating.

💡 The big idea: food in these countries is regional. The same word can mean different things by country — tortilla is a Spanish omelette in Spain, but a corn flatbread in Mexico.

2.2. The impersonal se (advanced — read for exposure)

Spanish often avoids "you" or "they" with se + verb. It means "one does it / it is done". You will see it constantly in recipes and articles.

  • Se prepara en una sartén grande.It is prepared in a large pan.
  • La paella se comparte con la familia.Paella is shared with the family.
  • Las arepas se comen con queso.Arepas are eaten with cheese.
💡 Pattern: se + tercera persona. Singular for a singular subject (se prepara), plural for a plural subject (se comen). Don't worry about producing it yet.

2.3. Talking about what you eat

To describe food habits, reuse verbs you already know: comer, beber, probar, gustar, preferir.

  • En mi país comemos mucho arroz.In my country we eat a lot of rice.
  • Me encanta la comida picante.I love spicy food.
  • Prefiero la carne a la parrilla.I prefer grilled meat.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences about food. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Say what a dish is made with:

La paella se prepara con arroz, pollo y mariscos.

Paella is made with rice, chicken and seafood.

2. Talk about a tradition:

El asado es una tradición en Argentina y Uruguay.

The asado is a tradition in Argentina and Uruguay.

3. Describe a flavour:

La salsa mexicana puede ser muy picante.

Mexican sauce can be very spicy.

4. Say what you like:

Me encantan las arepas con queso.

I love arepas with cheese.

5. Use the impersonal se:

Los tacos se comen con cilantro y salsa.

Tacos are eaten with coriander and sauce.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends argue, kindly, about food.

Diego: ¿Has probado la comida picante de verdad?

Diego: Have you tried really spicy food?

Rosa: Sí, en México. Allí todo se come con salsa.

Rosa: Yes, in Mexico. There everything is eaten with sauce.

Diego: Yo prefiero algo más suave. En Argentina comemos mucha carne a la parrilla.

Diego: I prefer something milder. In Argentina we eat a lot of grilled meat.

Rosa: ¡El asado es delicioso! Pero para mí, nada es mejor que unos tacos.

Rosa: Asado is delicious! But for me, nothing is better than tacos.

Diego: ¿Y qué bebes con la comida?

Diego: And what do you drink with the food?

Rosa: Agua o mate. El mate se toma a cualquier hora.

Rosa: Water or mate. Mate is drunk at any hour.

New words in the dialogue: probar (to try, to taste), suave (mild), delicioso/a (delicious), agua (water).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Spain: paella, tortilla (omelette), rice and seafood.
  • Mexico: tacos (corn tortillas), salsa picante, chocolate.
  • Venezuela / Colombia: arepas with cheese, meat or egg.
  • Argentina / Uruguay: asado on the parrilla, and mate.
  • Grammar: impersonal se prepara / se comen (advanced, for exposure).

5.2. Further reading — una receta sencilla

Context: A simple recipe for guacamole. Read it quietly:

El guacamole es una salsa mexicana muy fácil de preparar.

Guacamole is a Mexican sauce that is very easy to prepare.

Se necesitan aguacates, tomate, cebolla, cilantro y un poco de sal.

You need avocados, tomato, onion, coriander and a little salt.

Se cortan los ingredientes y se mezclan en un bol.

The ingredients are cut and mixed in a bowl.

Se come con tortillas de maíz o con pan.

It is eaten with corn tortillas or with bread.

New words: guacamole (guacamole), aguacate (avocado), sal (salt), mezclar (to mix).
