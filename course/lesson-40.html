LESSON 40 — FUTURE PERFECT & CONDITIONAL PERFECT

Lesson goals:
  • Use the future perfect (habré hecho) for actions finished before a future point — and for guesses about the past.
  • Use the conditional perfect (habría hecho) for what would have happened.
  • Read about a space probe and a sunken galleon: two races against time.
  • Pick up words: sonda, tripulación, órbita, hallazgo, yacimiento, pecio, naufragio.

Core vocabulary: habré, habrá, habría, habrían, sonda, tripulación, órbita, cometa, eclipse, despegue, despegar, alunizar, cápsula, nave, pronóstico, adelanto, deducir, hallazgo, yacimiento, pecio, tesoro, brújula, faro, resto, naufragio, muestra, pista, zarpar, naufragar, sumergir, rescatar, expedición
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): retrasar, sumergible, alunizaje

1. READING PASSAGE (DOS CARRERAS CONTRA EL TIEMPO)

Context: A science feature follows two expeditions: a probe heading for a comet, and divers searching for an 18th-century galleon off Cartagena.

(Both stories look back from the future — "by 2032, it will have arrived" — and imagine what would have happened otherwise.)

Para el año 2032, la sonda habrá llegado a la órbita del cometa.

By 2032, the probe will have reached the comet's orbit.

Para entonces, la tripulación de ingenieros ya habrá celebrado el despegue de la cápsula gemela.

By then, the crew of engineers will already have celebrated the launch of the twin capsule.

Sin el pronóstico del eclipse, la nave no habría despegado a tiempo.

Without the forecast of the eclipse, the craft would not have taken off on time.

Bajo el mar, los buzos creen que ya han encontrado una pista: un pecio con restos de un naufragio.

Under the sea, the divers believe they have already found a clue: a wreck with remains of a shipwreck.

El galeón habrá zarpado de Cartagena con un tesoro a bordo.

The galleon must have set sail from Cartagena with a treasure on board. (guess about the past)

La brújula y el faro de la bahía no habrían bastado para salvarlo de la tormenta.

The compass and the bay's lighthouse would not have been enough to save it from the storm.

Si la tormenta no lo hubiera sumergido, la expedición nunca habría existido.

If the storm hadn't sunk it, the expedition would never have existed.

Para fin de año, los arqueólogos habrán rescatado las primeras muestras del yacimiento.

By the end of the year, the archaeologists will have recovered the first samples from the site.

Cada hallazgo habrá merecido el esfuerzo: nadie habría apostado por este adelanto hace diez años.

Each find will have been worth the effort: nobody would have bet on this breakthrough ten years ago.

💡 Notice: habrá llegado looks forward and back at once ("will have arrived"); habría despegado imagines a past that never happened ("would have taken off"); habrá zarpado guesses about history ("must have set sail").

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Future perfect: haber in the future + participle

The future perfect (habré hecho) has two uses. First, an action that will be finished before a future moment, often with para… / para entonces / ya. Second, a conjecture about the past ("must have… / probably…").

habré, habrás, habrá, habremos, habréis, habrán + participle
  • Para el año 2032, la sonda habrá llegado.By 2032, the probe will have arrived.
  • Para fin de año, habrán rescatado las muestras.By the end of the year, they will have recovered the samples.
  • El galeón habrá zarpado de Cartagena.The galleon must have set sail from Cartagena. (guess)
  • ¿Dónde habrá quedado el pecio?Where can the wreck have ended up? (guess)

2.2. Conditional perfect: haber in the conditional + participle

The conditional perfect (habría hecho) means "would have done": unreal pasts, reproaches, and the result of type-3 si-clauses (full type 3 comes in Lesson 58; recognise it here).

habría, habrías, habría, habríamos, habríais, habrían + participle
  • La nave no habría despegado a tiempo.The craft would not have taken off on time.
  • Nadie habría apostado por este adelanto.Nobody would have bet on this breakthrough.
  • Si la tormenta no lo hubiera sumergido, la expedición nunca habría existido.If the storm hadn't sunk it, the expedition would never have existed.
  • Habrías podido avisar a la tripulación.You could have warned the crew. (reproach)

2.3. Telling the two apart

  • Habrá alunizado antes del eclipse.It will have landed on the moon before the eclipse. (future perfect: a plan)
  • Habría alunizado antes del eclipse.It would have landed on the moon before the eclipse. (conditional perfect: it didn't happen)
⚠️ Common mistake: habrá hecho (future perfect) is about the future or a guess — never use it for a simple completed past. For "he did it yesterday", use the preterite: lo hizo ayer.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences with compound tenses. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Finished before a future point:

Para fin de año, habrán rescatado las muestras.

By the end of the year, they will have recovered the samples.

2. A guess about the past:

El galeón habrá zarpado de Cartagena.

The galleon must have set sail from Cartagena.

3. An unreal past:

La nave no habría despegado a tiempo.

The craft would not have taken off on time.

4. A reproach:

Habrías podido avisar a la tripulación.

You could have warned the crew.

5. Looking back from tomorrow:

Para entonces, ya habré deducido lo ocurrido.

By then, I will already have figured out what happened.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two journalists discuss the expeditions.

Ana: ¿Crees que habrán encontrado el pecio?

Ana: Do you think they will have found the wreck (by now)?

Bruno: Dicen que para mañana ya habrán rescatado el tesoro.

Bruno: They say that by tomorrow they will already have recovered the treasure.

Ana: ¿Y la sonda? Habría llegado antes sin el retraso.

Ana: And the probe? It would have arrived earlier without the delay.

Bruno: El eclipse habrá alterado el pronóstico, supongo.

Bruno: The eclipse must have changed the forecast, I suppose.

Ana: Cada hallazgo habrá merecido la pena.

Ana: Each find will have been worth it.

New words in the dialogue: retraso is an extension preview; alterar (to alter) appears here in passing.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Future perfect: haber in the future + participle. Finished-before-a-future-point (para entonces, ya habré…) and guesses about the past (habrá zarpado = must have set sail).
  • Conditional perfect: haber in the conditional + participle. What would have happened (habría despegado), including reproaches.
  • The participle never changes: habrá llegado, habrían rescatado.

5.2. Further reading — el cometa de la bahía

Context: A short note linking the sky and the sea. Read it quietly:

Para la noche del eclipse, la sonda ya habrá cruzado la órbita del cometa.

By the night of the eclipse, the probe will already have crossed the comet's orbit.

Los viejos del puerto dicen que el galeón habrá visto el mismo cometa antes de zarpar.

The old sailors say the galleon must have seen the same comet before setting sail.

Sin la brújula, el capitán no habría encontrado ni el faro ni la pista de vuelta.

Without the compass, the captain would have found neither the lighthouse nor the way back.

Tres siglos después, cada muestra del pecio habrá contado su historia.

Three centuries later, each sample from the wreck will have told its story.

Ninguna expedición habría soñado un hallazgo mejor.

No expedition could have dreamed of a better find.

New words: cruzar (to cross) appears here in passing.
