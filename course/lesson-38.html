LESSON 38 — SUBJUNCTIVE IN ADJECTIVE CLAUSES

Lesson goals:
  • Use the subjunctive when the thing or person you mention is unknown, undefined or non-existent: busco un trabajo que…, no hay nadie que….
  • Keep the indicative when the person or thing is specific and known: tengo un trabajo que…, conozco a alguien que….
  • Read about looking for work and a better life.
  • Pick up words: empleo, oferta, salario, requisito, entrevista, solicitar.

Core vocabulary: permitir, alguien, ninguno / ningún, computadora / ordenador, motivar, convencer, tipo, oferta, poco/a, empleo, ofrecer, salario, sueldo, tranquilidad, existir, fórmula, requisito, currículum, entrevista, habilidad, puesto, solicitar, contrato, jornada, teletrabajo, candidato/a, beneficio, flexible, a tiempo completo
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): vacante, media jornada

1. READING PASSAGE (BUSCO UN TRABAJO QUE ME PERMITA AYUDAR)

Context: A young professional describes the kind of job and city she is looking for. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Every time she imagines something she does not have yet, the verb goes into the subjunctive.)

Busco un trabajo que me permita ayudar a la gente.

I'm looking for a job that allows me to help people.

Necesito una ciudad que tenga buenos hospitales.

I need a city that has good hospitals.

Quiero conocer a alguien que hable español conmigo.

I want to meet someone who speaks Spanish with me.

No hay nadie que quiera trabajar aquí por tan poco dinero.

There's nobody who wants to work here for so little money.

¿Conoces a alguien que sepa arreglar computadoras?

Do you know anyone who knows how to fix computers?

Busco algo que me motive cada mañana.

I'm looking for something that motivates me every morning.

No encuentro ningún curso que me convenza.

I can't find any course that convinces me.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. What an adjective clause is

An adjective clause is a relative clause (que…) that describes a noun, called the antecedent. Whether the verb takes the subjunctive depends on how real that antecedent is.

noun + que + subjunctive (unknown / non-existent)  ·  noun + que + indicative (known / specific)

2.2. Unknown or non-existent → subjunctive

  • Busco un trabajo que me permita ayudar.I'm looking for a job that allows me to help. (the job isn't found yet)
  • No hay nadie que quiera trabajar aquí.There's nobody who wants to work here. (nobody exists)
  • ¿Conoces a alguien que sepa arreglar computadoras?Do you know anyone who knows how to fix computers? (any person, undefined)
  • No encuentro ningún curso que me convenza.I can't find any course that convinces me.
💡 Words that often open a subjunctive clause: alguien / nadie / algo / nada / algún / ningún / un… que… / buscamos… que….

2.3. Known or specific → indicative

  • Tengo un trabajo que me permite ayudar.I have a job that allows me to help. (a real job)
  • Conozco a alguien que trabaja allí.I know someone who works there. (a specific person)
  • Hay gente que quiere ayudar.There are people who want to help. (they exist)

2.4. A useful pair to remember

  • Busco una persona que sepa inglés.I'm looking for a person who knows English. (any such person)
  • Busco a la persona que sabe inglés.I'm looking for the person who knows English. (a specific person)
⚠️ Common mistake: do not use the indicative after no hay nadie que. If the person does not exist, the verb is subjunctive: No hay nadie que quiera, not quiere.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences with undefined antecedents. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. A job not found yet:

Busco un trabajo que me permita ayudar a la gente.

I'm looking for a job that allows me to help people.

2. Nobody exists:

No hay nadie que quiera trabajar aquí por tan poco dinero.

There's nobody who wants to work here for so little money.

3. An undefined person:

¿Conoces a alguien que sepa arreglar computadoras?

Do you know anyone who knows how to fix computers?

4. A wish for motivation:

Busco algo que me motive cada mañana.

I'm looking for something that motivates me every morning.

5. Nothing convinces me:

No encuentro ningún curso que me convenza.

I can't find any course that convinces me.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends talk about a job search.

Diego: ¿Encontraste trabajo?

Diego: Did you find a job?

Nora: Todavía no. Busco algo que me guste de verdad.

Nora: Not yet. I'm looking for something I really like.

Diego: ¿Qué tipo de trabajo quieres?

Diego: What kind of job do you want?

Nora: Uno que me permita ayudar a la gente.

Nora: One that allows me to help people.

Diego: No conozco a nadie que tenga una oferta así, pero preguntaré.

Diego: I don't know anyone who has an offer like that, but I'll ask.

New words in the dialogue: tipo (type), oferta (offer), preguntar (to ask).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Undefined / non-existent antecedent → subjunctive: busco un trabajo que me permita…, no hay nadie que quiera….
  • Known / specific antecedent → indicative: tengo un trabajo que me permite…, conozco a alguien que trabaja….
  • Signals: alguien, nadie, algo, nada, algún, ningún, un… que….
  • This closes the six core subjunctive lessons of Module B1-1.

5.2. Further reading — buscando un lugar

Context: A short note about a personal search. Read it quietly:

Hay pocos empleos que ofrezcan un buen salario.

There are few jobs that offer a good salary.

Necesito un lugar que me dé tranquilidad.

I need a place that gives me peace of mind.

Busco personas que compartan mis ideas.

I'm looking for people who share my ideas.

No existe una fórmula que funcione para todos.

There is no formula that works for everyone.

Vi una vacante que ofrece media jornada.

I saw a vacancy that offers part time.

New words: compartir (to share), fórmula (formula).
