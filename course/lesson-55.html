LESSON 55 — MUSIC & CONTEMPORARY CULTURE

Lesson goals:
  • Read how Spanish-language music travels: flamenco, salsa, rock en español, reguetón — and the street parties in between.
  • Recycle por vs para (full uses) in musical contexts.
  • Recycle llevar + gerundio and seguir + gerundio.
  • Pick up words: cantaor, compás, duende, estribillo, perreo, dembow, verbena, movida.

Core vocabulary: cantaor, guitarrista, compás, palmas, taconeo, lamento, duende, jaleo, trovador, verso, estribillo, rima, letra, sonido, orquesta, coro, trompeta, tambor, acordeón, tararear, discoteca, bocina, verbena, chiringuito, botellón, movida, contracultura, underground, fanzine, perreo, dembow, reguetón, pegada, remezcla, samplear, playlist
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): karaoke, tocadiscos, autotune, viral

1. READING PASSAGE (DEL DUENDE AL DEMBOW)

Context: Five stops on a musical map: Andalusia, the Caribbean, Argentina, Puerto Rico, Madrid. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Reasons, causes and durations fill music writing — por and para are today's grammar.)

En Andalucía el flamenco no se escucha, se siente: un cantaor, una guitarra y palmas, y nadie habla porque todos escuchan.

In Andalusia flamenco isn't listened to, it's felt: a flamenco singer, a guitar and handclaps — and nobody talks because everyone listens.

El compás lo es todo: doce tiempos, taconeo y un lamento que viene de lejos, y para sentirlo hay que escuchar sin hablar.

Rhythm is everything: twelve beats, heel-tapping and a lament that comes from far away — and to feel it you have to listen without talking.

Paco de Lucía, el guitarrista, llevó el flamenco al mundo sin perder el compás, y Lorca llamó duende a lo mágico que sube por los pies y rompe la canción.

Paco de Lucía, the guitarist, took flamenco to the world without losing the beat — and Lorca called duende the magic that rises through the feet and breaks the song.

La fiesta sale a la calle en la verbena: orquesta, coro de vecinos y trompetas hasta tarde, y la orquesta toca para que bailen los vecinos.

The party spills into the street at the verbena: band, chorus of neighbours and trumpets until late — and the band plays so the neighbours dance.

En Cuba y Nueva York nació la salsa: trompetas, tambores y el azúcar de Celia Cruz, que se fue de Cuba y nunca volvió, pero su azúcar sigue en cada fiesta.

In Cuba and New York salsa was born: trumpets, drums and Celia Cruz's "sugar" — she left Cuba and never returned, but her sugar lives on at every party.

El rock cantó en español con Soda Stereo: versos raros y guitarras que todos conocen y todos cantan, con rima fácil de aprender.

Rock sang in Spanish with Soda Stereo: strange lyrics and guitars everyone knows and everyone sings, with easy-to-learn rhyme.

Y entonces llegó el dembow de Jamaica a Panamá y a Puerto Rico: nació el reguetón, y de allí fue al mundo entero, como el tango que viaja de Buenos Aires a Finlandia.

And then dembow arrived from Jamaica via Panama to Puerto Rico: reggaetón was born, and from there it went to the whole world — like tango travelling from Buenos Aires to Finland.

El tango también viaja: de Buenos Aires a Finlandia, donde hay tango finlandés.

Tango travels too: from Buenos Aires to Finland, where there is Finnish tango.

Gasolina tiene un estribillo que todos tararean sin saber inglés.

Gasolina has a chorus everyone hums without knowing English.

El perreo llenó las discotecas: bailar juntos y repetir la letra, y sin jaleo no hay fiesta.

Perreo filled the clubs: dancing together and repeating the lyrics — and without cheering there's no party.

Rosalía mezcló el flamenco con el reguetón y abrió un camino nuevo: la Motomami.

Rosalía mixed flamenco with reggaetón and opened a new path: the Motomami.

Bad Bunny llena estadios cantando la playa y su barrio, y sigue llenando estadios en todo el mundo porque tiene pegada.

Bad Bunny fills stadiums singing about the beach and his neighbourhood, and he keeps filling stadiums worldwide because he's catchy.

Del trovador medieval al playlist de hoy, cantar juntos como siempre, y pasa tu playlist a los amigos para seguir la música siempre, con sonido de bocina grande.

From the medieval troubadour to today's playlist, singing together as always — and pass your playlist to friends to keep the music going, with big-speaker sound.

En verano la música está en el chiringuito, y el DJ samplea un tambor viejo para la remezcla.

In summer the music is at the beach bar, and the DJ samples an old drum for the remix.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Por vs para, with music (recycled from L43)

Por looks back (cause, way through, exchange); para looks forward (purpose, recipient, deadline). The classic pair:

por = why / through / in exchange  ·  para = what for / for whom / by when
  • Toca por dinero, pero canta para su familia.He plays for money, but he sings for his family. (motive vs recipient)
  • Salieron a tocar por las calles para ganar dinero.They went out to play through the streets to earn money. (way through vs purpose)
  • Pasean por la playa para ver a los amigos.They stroll along the beach to see their friends.
  • La verbena es para todo el barrio.The street party is for the whole neighbourhood.

2.2. Llevar + gerundio: how long something has been going on (recycled from L44)

Llevar + time + gerundio measures an ongoing action; seguir + gerundio says it continues.

  • Lleva veinte años cantando.He has been singing for twenty years.
  • Llevan tres veranos tocando juntos.They have been playing together for three summers.
  • Sigue llenando estadios.He keeps filling stadiums.
💡 Spain-vs-LatAm note: the music travels with different names: Spain's ordenador is LatAm's computadora for making it, and the móvil (Spain) is the celular (LatAm) that plays it. Same songs, different words.

Paco de Lucía took flamenco to the world.

2. The dembow route:

El dembow llegó de Jamaica a Panamá.

Dembow came from Jamaica to Panama.

3. The hit:

Tiene pegada: todo el mundo la canta.

It's catchy: everyone sings it.

4. The long career:

Lleva veinte años cantando.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends prepare a party playlist — and defend their tastes.

Nico: Pon la remezcla: tiene más pegada.

Nico: Play the remix: it's catchier.

Sara: Bueno, pero después un verso con rima de verdad, que se aprenda solo.

Sara: OK, but afterwards a verse with real rhyme, one that learns itself.

Nico: El DJ samplea un tambor viejo y todos bailan.

Nico: The DJ samples an old drum and everyone dances.

Nico: Escucha el sonido de la bocina.

Nico: Listen to the speaker's sound.

Sara: Sin jaleo no hay fiesta.

Sara: Without cheering there's no party.

Sara: Y cerramos con karaoke sin autotune.

Sara: And we close with karaoke, no autotune.

Nico: Mi abuelo todavía tiene el tocadiscos. Lo traigo para el final.

Nico: My grandpa still has his record player. I'll bring it for the finale.

New words in the dialogue: remezcla (remix), pegada (catchiness), karaoke, tocadiscos (record player) — party vocabulary.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Por looks back (cause, way through): por dinero, por las calles; para looks forward (purpose, recipient): para ganar dinero, para su familia.
  • Llevar + gerundio measures time doing something; seguir + gerundio says it continues.
  • The map: flamenco feels before it is heard; salsa shouts sugar; rock learned Spanish; dembow became reggaetón; the street party never ends.

5.2. Further reading — la movida y el norte

Context: Two more scenes: Madrid's underground and Mexico's accordion. Read it quietly:

La movida fue la contracultura de Madrid: fanzines y noches sin fin.

La Movida was Madrid's counterculture: fanzines and endless nights.

Era underground: nadie ganaba dinero y todos tocaban por amor a la noche.

It was underground: nobody earned money and everyone played for the love of the night.

En el norte el acordeón es el rey: cumbias y rancheras para bailar juntos.

In the north the accordion is king: cumbias and rancheras for dancing together.

En verano la música está en el chiringuito, y el botellón reúne a cien jóvenes.

In summer the music is at the beach bar, and the botellón gathers a hundred young people.

Pasa tu playlist a los amigos: la música viaja por las manos.

Pass your playlist to your friends: music travels hand to hand.

New words: contracultura (counterculture), acordeón (accordion), chiringuito (beach bar) — from today's table.
