LESSON 51 — ART: FIVE LIVES IN FIVE PAINTINGS

Lesson goals:
  • Read about five artists who changed Spanish-language art: Velázquez, Goya, Picasso, Kahlo, Rivera.
  • Recycle the passive voice (fue pintado por…) and passive se (se puede ver).
  • Recycle relative clauses with cuyo and el cual.
  • Pick up words: cuadro, retrato, lienzo, pincel, luz, pared, mirada, obra.

Core vocabulary: cuadro, lienzo, pincel, pincelada, retrato, autorretrato, bodegón, óleo, acuarela, escultura, muralista, muralismo, paleta, mecenas, obra, maestro, mirada, fondo, sala, pared, luz, toro, caballo, sordo, pesadilla, bufón, menina, claroscuro, tenebrismo, composición, retratar, inmortalizar, cubismo, surrealismo, perspectiva, corte
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): vanguardia, autoría, franquismo

1. READING PASSAGE (CINCO VIDAS EN CINCO CUADROS)

Context: A gallery guide presents five key works of Spanish-language art. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Notice how often the text says who a work was painted by — the passive voice is the natural grammar of museums.)

En el Museo del Prado hay un cuadro que mira a quien lo mira: Las Meninas, del maestro Velázquez, que tuvo al rey como mecenas durante cuarenta años.

In the Prado Museum there is a painting that looks back at whoever looks at it: Las Meninas, by the master Velázquez, who had the king as his patron for forty years.

Velázquez está en el cuadro con el pincel en la mano, y la familia del rey observa la escena desde el fondo, mientras cada año lo miran miles de personas.

Velázquez is in the painting with his brush in his hand, and the king's family watches the scene from the background, while every year thousands of people look at it.

La niña del centro está con sus meninas y con dos bufones de la corte, los más libres del palacio.

The girl in the centre is with her ladies-in-waiting and with two court jesters, the freest figures in the palace.

Su claroscuro juega con la luz, y su composición mete al visitante dentro del cuadro, con una perspectiva que todavía sorprende.

His chiaroscuro plays with light, and his composition pulls the visitor inside the painting, with a perspective that still surprises.

A los cuarenta y seis años, Goya perdió el oído y quedó sordo para siempre.

At forty-six, Goya lost his hearing and remained deaf for the rest of his life.

Solo en su casa, llenó las paredes de pesadillas en negro: un Saturno que se come a su hijo es la más terrible.

Alone in his house, he filled the walls with black nightmares: a Saturn devouring his son is the most terrible.

Años antes había pintado El Tres de mayo, una obra que muestra un fusilamiento: el hombre de camisa blanca abre los brazos en el centro.

Years earlier he had painted The Third of May, a work showing an execution: the man in the white shirt opens his arms in the centre.

En 1937 le pidieron a Picasso un cuadro enorme para París, y pintó el Guernica en solo unas semanas, después de ver las fotos del bombardeo en los periódicos.

In 1937 Picasso was asked for an enormous painting for Paris, and he painted Guernica in just a few weeks, after seeing the photos of the bombing in the newspapers.

La madre con su hijo sin vida, el caballo herido y el toro que observa son un grito contra el bombardeo de un pueblo del norte.

The mother with her lifeless child, the wounded horse and the watching bull are a cry against the bombing of a northern town.

En el Guernica no hay colores: todo es negro, blanco y gris, como una foto de periódico, con el cubismo todavía vivo en cada figura.

In Guernica there are no colours: everything is black, white and grey, like a newspaper photo, with cubism still alive in every figure.

Al otro lado del mar, Frida Kahlo pintó su dolor en autorretratos pequeños, después de sobrevivir a un accidente terrible.

Across the sea, Frida Kahlo painted her pain in small self-portraits, after surviving a terrible accident.

En La columna rota se ve su cuerpo roto, pero su mirada sigue fuerte.

In The Broken Column her body appears broken, but her gaze remains strong.

Diego Rivera, su compañero, fue el muralista del pueblo: pintó a obreros y campesinos, y la historia de México, en las paredes de grandes edificios que el pueblo recuerda todavía.

Diego Rivera, her partner, was the people's mural painter: he painted workers and peasants, and the history of Mexico, on the walls of grand buildings that the people still remember.

También pintó en Estados Unidos para el pueblo: obreros y máquinas, del campo a la ciudad.

He also painted in the United States for the people: workers and machines, from the countryside to the city.

💡 Notice: fue pintado por Goya (it was painted by Goya) puts the work first and the artist second — exactly how museums speak. And un cuadro que mira a quien lo mira shows quien for people after a preposition.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The passive with ser (recycled from L41)

Art writing loves the passive: the work matters more than who holds the brush. Form: ser in the right tense + participle. The agent takes por.

ser + participle (+ por + artist)
  • Las Meninas fue pintado por Velázquez en 1656.Las Meninas was painted by Velázquez in 1656.
  • El Guernica fue encargado por el gobierno de la República.Guernica was commissioned by the government of the Republic.
  • Los murales de Rivera fueron pagados por el Estado mexicano.Rivera's murals were paid for by the Mexican state.

2.2. Passive se: what you see in every museum (recycled from L41)

Signs and guides prefer se + verb: no agent, just the fact. The verb agrees with the thing.

  • Aquí se venden reproducciones del cuadro.Reproductions of the painting are sold here.
  • En esa sala no se pueden hacer fotos.No photos can be taken in that room.
  • Se dice que Goya pintaba de noche, a la luz de las velas.They say Goya painted at night, by candlelight.
💡 Register note: fue pintado por… sounds formal and written (labels, articles); lo pintó Velázquez sounds spoken. Both are correct — museums mix them.

2.3. Relatives: cuyo and el cual (recycled from L34)

Cuyo (whose) links an owner to a thing and agrees with the thing owned, not the owner. El cual is a formal que, common in art writing after prepositions.

  • Goya, cuya sordera cambió su arte, pintó sus pesadillas en casa.Goya, whose deafness changed his art, painted his nightmares at home. (sordera is feminine → cuya)
  • Rivera, cuyos murales llenan palacios, quiso un arte para el pueblo.Rivera, whose murals fill palaces, wanted an art for the people. (murales is masculine plural → cuyos)
  • Fue el mecenas sin el cual Velázquez no habría llegado a la corte.He was the patron without whom Velázquez would never have reached the court.
⚠️ Common mistake: cuyo agrees with the possessed noun: el pintor cuya obra (the work is feminine), la pintora cuyos cuadros (the paintings are masculine plural).

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences about art and artists. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. About a painting:

Es un óleo sobre lienzo de 1656.

It is an oil on canvas from 1656.

2. Museum language:

La sala del Guernica siempre está llena.

The Guernica room is always full.

3. About style:

Goya pasó a una paleta oscura, casi negra.

Goya moved to a dark palette, almost black.

4. About a portrait:

El cuadro inmortalizó a la pequeña infanta.

The painting immortalised the little infanta.

5. About patronage:

El rey fue su mecenas durante cuarenta años.

The king was his patron for forty years.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends leave the museum and argue — politely — about their favourite painter.

Lucía: El Guernica me dejó sin palabras.

Lucía: Guernica left me speechless.

Lucía: ¿Cómo puede un lienzo tan grande no tener color?

Lucía: How can such a huge canvas have no colour?

Marco: A mí me gustó más la sala de Goya, cuyos retratos parecen fotografías.

Marco: I liked the Goya room more — his portraits look like photographs.

Lucía: ¿Viste su autorretrato de viejo? La mirada es durísima.

Lucía: Did you see his self-portrait as an old man? The gaze is brutally hard.

Marco: Fue pintado cuando ya estaba sordo. Por eso dicen que es tan oscuro.

Marco: It was painted when he was already deaf. That's why they say it's so dark.

Lucía: Pues yo me quedo con Kahlo: pequeña de tamaño, enorme de fondo.

Lucía: I'll stick with Kahlo: small in size, enormous in depth.

New words in the dialogue: retrato (portrait), sala (room), mirada (gaze) — all from today's table.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Passive with ser: fue pintado por Velázquez — the work goes first, the artist second.
  • Passive se: se venden reproducciones, no se pueden hacer fotos — museum language.
  • Relatives: cuya sordera (agrees with the possessed noun), el mecenas sin el cual….
  • Five lives: Velázquez paints the court from inside; Goya paints war and nightmares; Picasso paints a scream against bombing; Kahlo paints her own pain; Rivera paints history on walls.

5.2. Further reading — el joven Velázquez

Context: A short note about Velázquez before fame. Read it quietly:

De joven pintó bodegones con pan y huevos, cocinas humildes llenas de luz.

As a young man he painted still lifes with bread and eggs — humble kitchens full of light.

El rey, que sería su mecenas, aún no lo conocía.

The king, who would become his patron, did not know him yet.

Sus pinceladas sueltas, casi modernas, anuncian ya al gran maestro.

His loose, almost modern brushstrokes already announce the great master.

Toda su obra juega con la perspectiva: ¿quién mira a quién?

All his work plays with perspective: who is looking at whom?

Tres siglos después, el cubismo y el surrealismo romperían las reglas que él dominaba.

Three centuries later, cubism and surrealism would break the rules he had mastered.

New words: bodegón (still life), pincelada (brushstroke), maestro (master) — from today's table.
