LESSON 45 — AL-ÁNDALUS & MEDIEVAL SPAIN

Lesson goals:
  • Read about Córdoba around the year 961: the caliphate, its library and its mixed city life.
  • Re-use the pluperfect timeline (había construido) to order events across centuries.
  • Re-use relative clauses (que, donde, cuyo) to add detail to history.
  • Pick up words: mezquita, califato, convivencia, manuscrito, traductor, legado.

Core vocabulary: mezquita, califato, califa, emir, convivencia, tolerancia, tolerar, musulmán, cristiano, árabe, manuscrito, traductor, traducir, sabio, astrónomo, filósofo, médico, poeta, álgebra, cifra, ajedrez, acequia, regadío, huerta, zoco, judería, mudéjar, mozárabe, visigodo, invadir, conquistar, reino, muralla, esplendor, florecer, legado
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): aljibe, noria, patio

1. READING PASSAGE (CÓRDOBA, EL AÑO 961)

Context: A short history feature about Córdoba at its peak, when it was the largest city of Western Europe. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Watch the verb tenses: the story jumps between centuries, so the pluperfect marks what had happened before.)

Hace más de mil años, Córdoba era la ciudad más grande de Europa occidental, con calles iluminadas y baños públicos que Roma ya había perdido.

Over a thousand years ago, Córdoba was the largest city in Western Europe, with lit streets and public baths that Rome had already lost.

Era la capital del califato, el estado que gobernaba el califa Abderramán III, el gobernante más poderoso de su tiempo.

It was the capital of the caliphate, the state ruled by Caliph Abd al-Rahman III, the most powerful ruler of his time.

Dos siglos antes, el emir Abderramán I había construido la gran mezquita, un bosque de arcos donde la luz entraba entre cientos de columnas.

Two centuries earlier, Emir Abd al-Rahman I had built the great mosque, a forest of arches where light entered between hundreds of columns.

En sus calles, musulmanes y cristianos de lengua árabe vivían lado a lado, junto a mercaderes que venían de todo el Mediterráneo.

In its streets, Muslims and Christians of Arabic language lived side by side, together with merchants who came from all over the Mediterranean.

Esa convivencia no era perfecta, pero producía una tolerancia rara en Europa, que los reyes del norte no sabían tolerar en sus tierras.

That coexistence was not perfect, but it produced a tolerance rare in Europe, which the kings of the north did not know how to tolerate in their lands.

El califa había reunido miles de manuscritos en Córdoba: poesía, medicina y astronomía copiadas a mano por escribas que trabajaban día y noche.

The caliph had gathered thousands of manuscripts in Córdoba: poetry, medicine and astronomy copied by hand by scribes who worked day and night.

En su corte trabajaban sabios, médicos, poetas, filósofos y astrónomos cuyas obras se leerían en Europa durante siglos.

At his court worked scholars, doctors, poets, philosophers and astronomers whose works would be read in Europe for centuries.

Con el álgebra y las nuevas cifras, los mercaderes calculaban mejor, y con el ajedrez, que había llegado de Oriente, la corte se divertía.

With algebra and the new numerals, merchants calculated better, and with chess, which had arrived from the East, the court amused itself.

El agua llegaba por acequias a las huertas, y el regadío daba fruta todo el año en una tierra que antes había sido seca.

Water arrived through irrigation channels to the market gardens, and irrigation yielded fruit all year in a land that had previously been dry.

En el zoco, junto a la judería, se vendían perfumes, libros y joyas a compradores que regateaban en tres lenguas.

In the souk, next to the Jewish quarter, they sold perfumes, books and jewels to buyers who haggled in three languages.

Siglos después, los traductores de Toledo tradujeron esos libros al latín, y así el legado de Córdoba entró en las universidades de Europa.

Centuries later, the translators of Toledo translated those books into Latin, and thus Córdoba's legacy entered the universities of Europe.

Mientras tanto, los reinos cristianos del norte avanzaban poco a poco: invadían el sur y conquistaban ciudad tras ciudad.

Meanwhile, the Christian kingdoms of the north advanced little by little: they invaded the south and conquered city after city.

Las nuevas iglesias se levantaban sobre viejas murallas, y el arte mudéjar y mozárabe mezclaba los dos mundos en un solo edificio.

New churches rose over old walls, and Mudéjar and Mozarabic art mixed the two worlds in a single building.

Cuando los visigodos habían llegado, siglos antes, nadie imaginaba este esplendor; Córdoba florecía como pocas ciudades del mundo.

When the Visigoths had arrived, centuries earlier, nobody imagined this splendour; Córdoba flourished like few cities in the world.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The pluperfect timeline (recycled from L33)

History constantly compares two past moments. The pluperfect (había + participle) marks the one that happened first.

había + participle (first)  ·  preterite / imperfect (later)
  • El emir había construido la mezquita dos siglos antes.The emir had built the mosque two centuries earlier.
  • Cuando los reinos avanzaron, Córdoba ya había florecido.When the kingdoms advanced, Córdoba had already flourished.
  • Los traductores tradujeron libros que los sabios habían copiado.The translators translated books that the scholars had copied.

2.2. Relative clauses: que, donde, cuyo (recycled from L34)

Que = that/who, donde = where, cuyo = whose. They let one sentence carry two facts — the signature move of historical writing.

  • Era la capital del califato, el estado que gobernaba el califa.It was the capital of the caliphate, the state (that) the caliph ruled.
  • La mezquita, un bosque de arcos donde entraba la luz.The mosque, a forest of arches where the light entered.
  • Sabios cuyas obras se leerían durante siglos.Scholars whose works would be read for centuries. (cuyas agrees with obras.)
⚠️ Common mistake: cuyo agrees with the thing possessed, not the owner: el califa cuyas obras… is wrong if the works are feminine plural — it must be cuyas obras.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences with the pluperfect and relative clauses. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. The earlier event:

El emir había construido la mezquita dos siglos antes.

The emir had built the mosque two centuries earlier.

2. Whose works:

Eran sabios cuyas obras se leerían durante siglos.

They were scholars whose works would be read for centuries.

3. Where:

Era un bosque de arcos donde entraba la luz.

It was a forest of arches where the light entered.

4. Coexistence:

Esa convivencia producía una tolerancia rara en Europa.

That coexistence produced a tolerance rare in Europe.

5. The legacy:

Los traductores llevaron el legado de Córdoba a Europa.

The translators carried Córdoba's legacy to Europe.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two visitors stand inside the Mezquita of Córdoba with a guide.

Guía: Lo que ven aquí lo construyó el emir en el siglo VIII.

Guide: What you see here was built by the emir in the 8th century.

Lucía: ¿Y la catedral que está en el centro? ¿Cuándo la levantaron?

Lucía: And the cathedral in the centre? When was it raised?

Guía: Siglos después, cuando los reinos cristianos ya habían conquistado la ciudad.

Guide: Centuries later, when the Christian kingdoms had already conquered the city.

Marco: Me impresiona la convivencia: una mezquita y una catedral en un solo edificio.

Marco: The coexistence impresses me: a mosque and a cathedral in a single building.

Guía: Exacto. Este patio de naranjos era el lugar donde la gente se reunía antes de rezar.

Guide: Exactly. This orange-tree courtyard was the place where people gathered before praying.

New words in the dialogue: patio (courtyard), reunirse (to gather), rezar (to pray).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Córdoba ~961: capital of the califato; the great mezquita, the manuscritos and the convivencia of cultures.
  • Pluperfect timeline: había construido, había reunido = the earlier of two past events.
  • Relative clauses: que (that/who), donde (where), cuyo (whose).
  • Word families: tolerancia → tolerar; traductor → traducir; conquistar → la conquista.

5.2. Further reading — los traductores de Toledo

Context: After the Christian conquest of Toledo (1085), the city's books changed European history. Read it quietly:

Toledo había sido una ciudad del califato, llena de manuscritos que nadie en el norte sabía leer.

Toledo had been a city of the caliphate, full of manuscripts that nobody in the north knew how to read.

Los traductores, cristianos y judíos que hablaban árabe, tradujeron la medicina y el álgebra al latín.

The translators, Christians and Jews who spoke Arabic, translated medicine and algebra into Latin.

Con esas cifras nuevas y esos sabios antiguos, Europa aprendió a calcular, a curar y a mirar las estrellas.

With those new numerals and those ancient scholars, Europe learned to calculate, to heal and to watch the stars.

El agua de sus norias seguía subiendo a las huertas, pero el verdadero legado viajaba en los libros.

The water of its waterwheels kept rising to the gardens, but the true legacy travelled in books.

Sin los aljibes no había ciudad, decían; sin los libros, añadimos hoy, no habría universidad.

Without cisterns there was no city, they said; without books, we add today, there would be no university.

New words: escriba (scribe), estrella (star).
