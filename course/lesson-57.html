LESSON 57 — IMPERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE & TENSE SEQUENCES

Lesson goals:
  • Form the imperfect subjunctive: hablara / hablase, quisiera, pudiera, fuera.
  • Master tense sequences: quería que vinieras (past main verb → imperfect subjunctive).
  • Meet si + imperfect subjunctive: si pudiera volver…
  • Read an emigrant's letter full of wishes about the past.
  • Pick up words: añoranza, morriña, desarraigo, arraigo, remesa, terruño, reencuentro.

Core vocabulary: quisiera, pudiera, hablara, hablase, vinieras, fuera, fuese, añoranza, morriña, desarraigo, arraigo, remesa, retorno, terruño, reencuentro, despedida, nostalgia, sacrificio, integrarse, consuelo, consolar, extrañar, lejanía, patria, llamada, enviar, anhelar, rogar, suplicar, adaptarse, resignarse, paisano, compatriota, melancolía, videollamada, valija
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): ultramar, repatriar, repatriado, anochecer

1. READING PASSAGE (SI PUDIERA VOLVER)

Context: Elena, an Ecuadorian emigrant living in Madrid for six years, writes a letter to her mother. She remembers the goodbye, describes her new life, and dreams of return. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Wishes about the past fill this letter — she wanted him to come, if I could go back… is today's grammar.)

Querida mamá: te escribo desde un piso pequeño de Madrid, y si pudiera volver atrás, reviviría nuestra despedida en el aeropuerto.

Dear Mom: I'm writing from a small flat in Madrid, and if I could go back, I would relive our goodbye at the airport.

Me pediste que fuera fuerte, aunque yo solo quisiera quedarme contigo en casa.

You asked me to be strong, although all I wanted was to stay home with you.

El avión despegó de noche, y miré por la ventanilla como si fuera la última vez que veía mi patria, con sus montañas verdes y su cielo tan alto.

The plane took off at night, and I looked out of the little window as if it were the last time I would see my homeland, with its green mountains and its sky so high.

Aquí la nostalgia tiene otro nombre: los gallegos la llaman morriña, y ahora entiendo esa añoranza que no se cura con nada.

Here nostalgia has another name: Galicians call it morriña, and now I understand that longing which nothing cures.

Al principio todo era desarraigo: otra comida, otro frío y ni una cara conocida; ni siquiera el español sonaba igual en la calle.

At first everything was uprootedness: different food, different cold, and not one familiar face; not even Spanish sounded the same in the street.

Poco a poco encontré trabajo, aprendí las costumbres, y entendí que para adaptarse hay que perder la vergüenza y preguntar todo dos veces.

Little by little I found work, I learned the customs, and I understood that to adapt you have to lose your shyness and ask everything twice.

Eso es el arraigo, me dijo una amiga que trabaja con inmigrantes: no olvidar de dónde vienes, sino vivir tranquila donde estás, porque hasta un repatriado puede volver a empezar.

That's putting down roots, a friend who works with immigrants told me: not forgetting where you come from, but living calmly where you are — because even a returnee can start over.

Cada mes logro enviar una remesa pequeña, y me dices que con eso pagan la luz, los remedios del abuelo y los libros de mis hermanos pequeños.

Every month I manage to send a small remittance, and you tell me that with it they pay for the electricity, Grandpa's medicines and my little brothers' schoolbooks.

No es mucho, pero es mi sacrificio con amor: trabajar horas extra para que ustedes vivan mejor.

It's not much, but it's my sacrifice with love: working extra hours so that you live better.

Un paisano del centro sabe consolar cuando estoy triste, y con un compatriota cerca la lejanía duele menos.

A fellow countryman at the migrants' center knows how to comfort me when I'm sad, and with a compatriot nearby, distance hurts less.

Hablamos de la tierra, del terruño que dejamos, y soñamos con el reencuentro de diciembre; a veces lloramos un poco, pero de alegría.

We talk about our land, about the native soil we left behind, and we dream of the reunion in December; sometimes we cry a little, but with joy.

Mis jefes querían que vinieras a visitarme en verano, y tuve que rogarles que te pagaran el pasaje.

My bosses wanted you to come visit me in the summer, and I had to beg them to pay for your ticket.

Llegué a suplicarles que entendieran: una madre no debería viajar a ultramar sola a su edad.

I went so far as to plead with them to understand: a mother shouldn't travel overseas alone at her age.

Si el consulado hablara más claro, todo sería fácil; pero cada papel tarda meses y cada respuesta es un misterio que nadie sabe explicar.

If the consulate spoke more clearly, everything would be easy; but every document takes months and every answer is a mystery nobody can explain.

Mi abogado dice que a nadie van a repatriar sin abogado, pero que si yo hablase con el juez en persona, mis papeles saldrían antes de la fiesta de diciembre.

My lawyer says nobody will be repatriated without a lawyer, but that if I spoke to the judge in person, my papers would come through before the December party.

Hacemos una videollamada cada domingo, y me río contigo sin dejar de extrañar tu voz ni de anhelar tu comida.

We make a video call every Sunday, and I laugh with you while still missing your voice and longing for your cooking.

Guardo tu valija vieja bajo la cama, y cuando llega la melancolía del anochecer, la abro y huele a casa, a café recién hecho y a ropa limpia.

I keep your old suitcase under the bed, and when the nightfall melancholy arrives, I open it and it smells of home — of freshly made coffee and clean clothes.

Solo te pido un consuelo: que me esperes con paciencia, porque nadie debería resignarse a vivir lejos para siempre, y yo creo en el retorno; algún día comeremos juntas otra vez.

I only ask one comfort of you: wait for me patiently, because nobody should resign themselves to living far away forever — and I believe in return; one day we'll eat together again.

Dicen que integrarse es dejar de ser de fuera, pero yo creo que es ser de dos lugares a la vez.

They say that integrating is stopping being an outsider, but I think it's being from two places at once.

💡 Notice: past wishes open past subjunctives — querían que vinieras, me pediste que fuera, tuve que rogarles que pagaran. Whenever the main verb is past, the que-clause steps one tense back. That is today's whole game.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The form: third-person plural preterite minus -ron, plus -ra / -se

Take the ellos form of the preterite (hablaron, quisieron, pudieron, fueron), drop -ron, and add the endings -ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -rais, -ran. Every verb works the same way — even the irregular ones. There is a twin set with -se (hablase, quisiese) that means exactly the same.

ellos preterite − -ron + -ra / -se → quisiera / quisiese, pudiera / pudiese, hablara / hablase
  • Yo solo quisiera quedarme contigo.All I wanted was to stay with you. (querer → quisieron → quisiera)
  • Si pudiera volver atrás, lo reviviría todo.If I could go back, I would relive it all. (poder → pudieron → pudiera)
  • Si el consulado hablara más claro… / …si yo hablase con el juez…If the consulate spoke more clearly… / …if I spoke to the judge… (-ra and -se are twins)
  • Como si fuera la última vez. / Como si fuese la última vez.As if it were the last time. (ser/ir → fueron → fuera/fuese)

2.2. Tense sequences: past main verb → imperfect subjunctive

You know the present pattern (quiero que vengas). When the main verb moves to the past, the subjunctive moves to the imperfect subjunctive: quería que vinieras. Wishes, orders, requests and feelings about things that were present-or-future at that past moment.

main verb in past + que + imperfect subjunctive → quería que vinieras
  • Mis jefes querían que vinieras en verano.My bosses wanted you to come in summer.
  • Me pediste que fuera fuerte.You asked me to be strong.
  • Tuve que rogarles que te pagaran el pasaje.I had to beg them to pay for your ticket.
  • Llegué a suplicarles que entendieran.I went so far as to plead with them to understand.
💡 Spain-vs-LatAm note: -ra and -se are both correct everywhere — no variety is wrong. In speech, -ra (hablara, quisiera) is more common on both sides of the ocean. And quisiera has a second life everywhere as a polite I would like: quisiera pedirle un favor (I would like to ask you a favour).

2.3. First taste of si + imperfect subjunctive

Unreal si-clauses about the present or future take the imperfect subjunctive, and the result takes the conditional. Full unreal and mixed conditionals come in L58; for now, just taste the pattern.

si + imperfect subjunctive + conditional → si pudiera, volvería
  • Si pudiera volver atrás, lo reviviría todo.If I could go back, I would relive it all.
  • Si el consulado hablara más claro, todo sería fácil.If the consulate spoke more clearly, everything would be easy.
  • Quisiera pedirle un favor.I would like to ask you a favour. (polite quisiera, no si needed)
⚠️ Common mistakes: after si, never use the conditional: *si podría, *si hablaría are wrong — always si pudiera, si hablara. The -ía ending lives only in the result (volvería, sería), never in the si-clause.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences of wishes and politeness. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. The polite wish:

Quisiera pedirle un favor.

I would like to ask you a favour.

2. The impossible return:

Si pudiera, volvería mañana.

If I could, I'd come back tomorrow.

3. The past request:

Me pidió que viniera en verano.

She asked me to come in summer.

4. As if:

Como si fuera la última vez.

As if it were the last time.

5. The promised call:

Te debo una llamada larga el domingo.

I owe you a long call on Sunday.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Elena and her friend Marta, in a café in Madrid, talk about the lawyer and the papers.

Marta: Elena, ¿ya hablaste con el abogado?

Marta: Elena, did you talk to the lawyer yet?

Elena: Sí, y me dijo que si yo hablase con el juez, todo iría más rápido.

Elena: Yes, and he told me that if I spoke to the judge, everything would go faster.

Marta: ¿Y si le escribieras una carta antes de la cita?

Marta: What if you wrote him a letter before the appointment?

Elena: Quisiera, pero me pidió que llevara testigos.

Elena: I'd like to, but he asked me to bring witnesses.

Marta: Yo te acompaño. Y esta noche hacemos una videollamada con tu mamá.

Marta: I'll go with you. And tonight we'll make a video call with your mom.

Elena: Gracias. Soñar con el reencuentro me da consuelo.

Elena: Thanks. Dreaming of the reunion comforts me.

Marta: Y cuando lleguen los papeles, celebramos sin nostalgia.

Marta: And when the papers arrive, we'll celebrate with no nostalgia.

No new words here — the dialogue reuses videollamada (video call), reencuentro (reunion) and consuelo (comfort) from today's table.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • The form: ellos preterite minus -ron + -ra/-se: quisiera, pudiera, hablara/hablase, fuera. Twins, both correct everywhere.
  • Tense sequences: present main verb → present subjunctive (quiero que vengas); past main verb → imperfect subjunctive (quería que vinieras).
  • Si-taste: si + imperfect subjunctive + conditional (si pudiera, volvería) — never a conditional after si. Full conditionals arrive in L58.

5.2. Further reading — la fiesta del centro

Context: Elena describes the migrants' center party in December. Read it quietly:

En el centro de paisanos organizan una fiesta cada diciembre para no extrañar tanto.

At the fellow-countrymen's center they organize a party every December so as not to miss home so much.

Cada compatriota trae un plato de su tierra y cuenta una historia del terruño.

Each compatriot brings a dish from their land and tells a story of their native soil.

Este año quieren que yo cante, aunque yo quisiera solo escuchar.

This year they want me to sing, although I'd just like to listen.

Si lloviera, la harían dentro; si hiciera frío, encenderían la estufa.

If it rained, they'd hold it inside; if it were cold, they'd light the heater.

Y a medianoche brindarían por el retorno, sin resignarse nunca.

And at midnight they'd toast the return, never resigning themselves.

No new words — it reuses extrañar (to miss), compatriota (compatriot), terruño (native soil), quisiera, retorno (return) and resignarse from today's table.
