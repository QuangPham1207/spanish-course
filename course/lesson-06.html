LESSON 06 — THE PRESENT TENSE

Lesson goals:
  • Learn the subject pronouns: yo, tú, él/ella, nosotros, vosotros, ellos.
  • Conjugate regular -ar / -er / -ir verbs in the present.
  • Say what you and others do every day: hablo, comes, escribe…
  • Make negatives with no: No bebo café.

Core vocabulary: yo, él/ella, nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas, ustedes, hablar, comer, beber, leer, escribir, aprender, comprender, no, mucho, poco
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): inglés, periódico, café, té, noche, frase, nuevo/a, bien, todos los días, idioma, escuela, divertido

1. READING PASSAGE (EVERY DAY)

Context: A student describes what he and the people around him do. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(Read top to bottom — the Spanish is enough to get the gist.)

Soy estudiante de español.

I'm a student of Spanish.

Estudio todos los días: leo un libro, escribo frases y aprendo palabras nuevas.

I study every day: I read a book, write sentences and learn new words.

Mi amiga Ana es médica; trabaja en un hospital y aprende inglés por la noche.

My friend Ana is a doctor; she works in a hospital and learns English at night.

Mi hermano bebe café y lee el periódico.

My brother drinks coffee and reads the newspaper.

Yo no bebo café; bebo té.

I don't drink coffee; I drink tea.

En la clase, nosotros comprendemos bien el español.

In class, we understand Spanish well.

¿Hablas español? — Sí, hablo un poco de español.

Do you speak Spanish? — Yes, I speak a little Spanish.

Tú vives en Vietnam y yo vivo en España.

You live in Vietnam and I live in Spain.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Subject pronouns

Spanish has a pronoun for every person. You already know tú and usted (Lesson 01).

PersonPronounEnglish
1st singularyoI
2nd singulartú / ustedyou (informal / formal)
3rd singularél / ellahe / she
1st pluralnosotros / nosotraswe
2nd pluralvosotros / vosotras (Spain)you all (informal)
3rd pluralellos / ellas / ustedesthey / you all
  • Spanish usually leaves the pronoun out, because the verb ending already says who: Hablo español = "I speak Spanish".
  • Use it only for emphasis or contrast: Yo bebo té; él bebe café = "I drink tea; he drinks coffee".
💡 Spain vs Latin America: Spain has vosotros (informal plural) with its own verb endings. Latin America uses ustedes for both formal and informal "you all".

2.2. Regular verbs in the present

Every infinitive ends in -ar, -er or -ir. Take off that ending and add the person endings below. The three groups differ only in we and you all.

The endings, summarized:

-ar: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an  ·  -er: -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en  ·  -ir: -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en
  • Hablo español y un poco de inglés.I speak Spanish and a little English.
  • Ana come en el hospital.Ana eats at the hospital.
  • Vivimos en Hanói.We live in Hanoi.
  • Los estudiantes aprenden rápido.The students learn fast. (advanced)
⚠️ Three verbs to watch (they are irregular, but very common): ser (soy, eres, es…), estar (estoy, estás, está…) and tener (tengo, tienes, tiene…). You met them in Lessons 01-03.

2.3. Negatives with no

To make a sentence negative, put no right before the verb:

no + verb · No bebo café.
  • No bebo café.I don't drink coffee.
  • Ana no trabaja hoy.Ana isn't working today.
  • No comprendo.I don't understand.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences you can use from day one. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. Say what you do every day:

Estudio español todos los días.

I study Spanish every day.

2. Ask and answer about a language:

¿Hablas español? — Sí, hablo un poco.

Do you speak Spanish? — Yes, I speak a little.

3. State a preference with a negative:

Yo no bebo café; bebo té.

I don't drink coffee; I drink tea.

4. Describe a habit:

Mi hermano lee el periódico.

My brother reads the newspaper.

5. Talk about a group:

Nosotros escribimos frases y aprendemos palabras nuevas.

We write sentences and learn new words.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two students talk about learning languages.

Ana: ¿Hablas español, Kenji?

Ana: Do you speak Spanish, Kenji?

Kenji: Sí, un poco. Estudio español todos los días.

Kenji: Yes, a little. I study Spanish every day.

Ana: ¿Lees libros en español?

Ana: Do you read books in Spanish?

Kenji: Sí, leo un libro y escribo frases nuevas.

Kenji: Yes, I read a book and write new sentences.

Ana: ¿Y tu hermano? ¿Estudia también?

Ana: And your brother? Does he study too?

Kenji: No, él no estudia español. Trabaja y aprende inglés.

Kenji: No, he doesn't study Spanish. He works and learns English.

Ana: ¡Qué interesante!

Ana: How interesting!

New words in the dialogue: estudia (he/she studies), trabaja (he/she works).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Regular present endings: -ar (hablo, hablas, habla…), -er (como, comes, come…), -ir (vivo, vives, vive…).
  • The pronoun is usually left out; use it for emphasis: Yo bebo té; él bebe café.
  • Negation: no + verb → No comprendo.
  • vosotros (Spain) vs ustedes (Latin America) for "you all".
  • Three common irregulars you already know: ser, estar, tener.

5.2. Further reading — learning a language

Context: A short note on how people learn languages. Read it quietly:

Muchas personas aprenden español en la escuela.

Many people learn Spanish at school.

Leer todos los días es muy importante.

Reading every day is very important.

No necesitas mucho tiempo: quince minutos al día son suficientes.

You don't need much time: fifteen minutes a day is enough.

Cuando comprendes una palabra nueva, aprendes poco a poco.

When you understand a new word, you learn little by little.

Un idioma no es difícil: es divertido.

A language isn't difficult: it's fun.

New words: escuela (school), necesitar (to need), suficiente (enough), poco a poco (little by little), idioma (language), divertido (fun).
