LESSON 60 — THE MANY VALUES OF SE

Lesson goals:
  • Tell apart reflexive se (se sienta) and reciprocal se (se saludan, se pelean).
  • Read passive se (se alquila, se venden) and impersonal se (se vive bien).
  • Master accidental se (se me cayeron las llaves) and aspectual se (se comió todo, se durmió).
  • Read about one week in an apartment building where nobody escapes se.
  • Pick up words: vecindad, rellano, zaguán, tendedero, derrama, conserje, trastero.

Core vocabulary: vecino, vecindad, portero, escalera, ascensor, rellano, conserje, cuota, avería, fontanero, cerrajero, inquilino, casero, trastero, zaguán, tendedero, derrama, llave, quejarse, sentarse, saludarse, pelearse, escribirse, alquilarse, venderse, caerse, romperse, perderse, comerse, beberse, dormirse, acordarse, olvidarse, mirarse, cortarse
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): consorcio, moroso, felpudo, reja

1. READING PASSAGE (UNA SEMANA EN LA VECINDAD)

Context: A week in an old apartment building in Mexico City. Almost every sentence hides a different se — reflexive, reciprocal, passive, impersonal, accidental or aspectual. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(One tiny word, six jobs. By the end of this text you will have met them all.)

En nuestra vecindad viven cuarenta vecinos en tres escaleras, y aquí todo el mundo se conoce por su nombre.

In our apartment block live forty neighbours across three stairwells, and here everyone knows each other by name.

Por la mañana, el portero abre el zaguán a las siete, sube en el ascensor hasta la azotea y baja andando para ver cada rellano.

In the morning, the doorman opens the entrance hall at seven, rides the lift up to the roof terrace and walks down to check each landing.

Los inquilinos del tercero se saludan todos los días en el rellano, mientras el casero solo viene cuando hay que pagar la cuota.

The tenants on the third floor greet each other every day on the landing, while the landlord only shows up when the monthly fee is due.

Se alquila el piso pequeño del primero, y ayer lo dijo el portero en la escalera, y todos los vecinos ya lo saben.

The small flat on the first floor is for rent — the doorman said so yesterday on the stairs, and all the neighbours already know.

En el quinto se venden dos trasteros llenos de cosas viejas, porque la familia ya vive en otra ciudad.

On the fifth floor two storage rooms full of old things are for sale, because the family already lives in another city.

En esta casa se vive bien: a las diez de la noche se apaga la luz de la escalera y nadie hace ruido.

Life is good in this building: at ten at night the stairwell light goes off and nobody makes noise.

El lunes salió una gotera en el segundo, y el fontanero vino dos días después a ver la avería.

On Monday a leak appeared on the second floor, and the plumber came two days later to look at the breakdown.

A la vecina del cuarto se le cayeron las llaves dentro del ascensor, y hubo que llamar al cerrajero.

The neighbour on the fourth floor dropped her keys inside the lift (the keys fell on her), and they had to call the locksmith.

Al niño del primero se le perdió la llave de la azotea, y su madre dice que siempre se le pierde todo.

The boy on the first floor lost the roof-terrace key (it got lost on him), and his mother says everything always gets lost on him.

A mí se me olvidaron las llaves dentro de casa, y el conserje me abrió la puerta con su llave.

I forgot my keys inside the house (they slipped my mind), and the caretaker opened the door for me with his key.

Mi vecino dice que nunca se le olvida nada, pero ayer se acordó del pan cuando las tiendas ya estaban cerradas.

My neighbour says he never forgets anything, but yesterday he remembered the bread only when the shops were already closed.

Las dos hermanas del sexto se pelean cada semana por el tendedero de la azotea: las dos quieren usarlo a la misma hora.

The two sisters on the sixth floor fight each other every week over the roof-terrace clothesline: both want to use it at the same time.

En cambio, los abuelos del bajo se miran y se sientan cada tarde en el zaguán a hablar con la gente.

The grandparents on the ground floor, on the other hand, look at each other and sit down every afternoon in the entrance hall to chat with people.

El domingo el conserje se durmió después de comer y nadie pudo entrar en casa hasta las tres de la tarde.

On Sunday the caretaker fell asleep after lunch and nobody could get into the building until three in the afternoon.

En la fiesta del barrio, la vecina del segundo se comió tres platos de paella y se bebió todo el vino de la mesa.

At the neighbourhood party, the neighbour from the second floor ate up three plates of paella and drank up all the wine on the table.

Los vecinos se quejan de la derrama de este año, porque la cuota de cada familia es más alta.

The neighbours are complaining about this year's special building levy, because each family's fee is higher.

A la niña del segundo se le rompió un plato en la escalera, y su hermano se cortó la mano.

The little girl on the second floor broke a plate on the stairs (it broke on her), and her brother cut his hand.

Por las noches, los jóvenes del edificio se escriben para bajar a la calle, y así nadie se siente solo en esta vecindad.

At night, the young people in the building write to each other to go down to the street, so nobody feels alone in this block.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Reflexive se and reciprocal se: to oneself vs each other

Reflexive se means the subject acts on itself (ella se sienta — she sits herself down). Reciprocal se needs a plural subject and means "each other" — the same form, but two people acting on each other.

Reflexive (one person): sentarse, dormirse — ella se sienta · Reciprocal (two or more): saludarse, pelearse — ellas se saludan
  • Los abuelos se sientan en el zaguán.The grandparents sit down in the entrance hall. (reflexive)
  • El conserje se durmió después de comer.The caretaker fell asleep after lunch. (reflexive: change of state)
  • Se saludan en el rellano.They greet each other on the landing. (reciprocal)
  • Se pelean por el tendedero.They fight each other over the clothesline. (reciprocal)
  • Se miran y se sientan a hablar.They look at each other and sit down to chat. (both in one line)

2.2. Passive se and impersonal se: nobody in particular

Passive se hides the agent: the verb agrees with the thing (se venden trasteros — storage rooms are for sale). Impersonal se has no subject at all and always stays singular (se vive bien — life is good / one lives well).

Passive: verb agrees with the thing (venderse → se venden trasteros) · Impersonal: always 3rd singular (alquilarse → se alquila el piso; se vive bien)
  • Se alquila el piso del primero.The first-floor flat is for rent. (passive se, singular)
  • Se venden dos trasteros.Two storage rooms are for sale. (passive se, plural)
  • Aquí se vive bien.Life is good here. (impersonal se — literally "one lives well")
  • En casa se habla español.Spanish is spoken at home. (impersonal se)
💡 Spain-vs-LatAm note: close friends in Spain say os veis (you see each other), in Latin America se ven — because ustedes replaces vosotros. All six values of se in this lesson are identical in every country.

2.3. Accidental se and aspectual se: oops vs all gone

Accidental se apologizes for you: se + me/te/le… + a verb that agrees with the thing lost or broken (se me cayeron las llaves — it wasn't my fault!). Aspectual se eats the whole thing: it adds "completely, up" (se comió todo, se bebió todo, se durmió).

Accident: se + me/te/le… + verb agreeing with the thing (caerse, olvidarse, romperse, perderse) · Totality: verb + se (comerse, beberse, dormirse)
  • Se me cayeron las llaves.I dropped my keys. (literally "the keys fell on me")
  • Se le rompió un plato.She broke a plate. (literally "a plate broke on her")
  • Se comió tres platos de paella.She ate up three plates of paella. (aspectual: completely)
  • Se bebió todo el vino.He drank up all the wine. (aspectual: totally)
  • Ayer se acordó del pan.Yesterday he remembered the bread. (acordarse: memory "comes back")
⚠️ Common mistakes: se venden pisos means "flats are for sale" — not "the flats sell themselves". And never drop the dative in accidents: se me cayeron (my keys, poor me), not se cayeron (they just fell).

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five se sentences. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. The for-rent sign:

Se alquila el piso del primero.

The first-floor flat is for rent.

2. The classic excuse:

Se me cayeron las llaves.

I dropped my keys. (The keys fell on me.)

3. All gone:

Se comió todo lo del plato.

She ate up everything on her plate.

4. Good neighbours:

Los vecinos se saludan todos los días.

The neighbours greet each other every day.

5. Life is good:

Aquí se vive muy bien.

Life is very good here. (One lives very well here.)

💡 Optional (skip if you like): whenever you feel like it, read all five out loud once. Not required.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two neighbours meet on the stairs and talk about the late payer on the fifth floor.

Marta: Buenos días. ¿Ya pagó la cuota el moroso del quinto?

Marta: Morning. Has the late payer on the fifth paid his fee yet?

Luis: No. Dice que se le olvidó pagar.

Luis: No. He says he forgot to pay. (Paying slipped his mind.)

Marta: Siempre se le olvida todo. El lunes se le cayeron las llaves y llamó al cerrajero.

Marta: He always forgets everything. On Monday he dropped his keys and called the locksmith.

Luis: Y ayer se quejó de la gotera, pero la avería está en su casa.

Luis: And yesterday he complained about the leak — but the fault is inside his own flat.

Marta: Mira su felpudo nuevo: dice "aquí se vive bien".

Marta: Look at his new doormat: it says "life is good here".

Luis: Pues dile que se siente en el zaguán y hable con el conserje.

Luis: Then tell him to sit down in the entrance hall and talk to the caretaker.

Marta: Esta tarde hablo con él en la escalera.

Marta: I'll talk to him this afternoon on the stairs.

New words in the dialogue: moroso (late payer), felpudo (doormat) — both extension words from today's table.

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Reflexive: se sientan, se durmió, se cortó — the subject acts on itself.
  • Reciprocal: se saludan, se pelean, se miran, se escriben — plural subject, "each other".
  • Passive & impersonal: se alquila, se venden (verb agrees with the thing) vs se vive bien (always singular, no subject).
  • Accidental: se me cayeron, se me olvidaron, se le perdió, se le rompió — with me/te/le…, nobody's fault.
  • Aspectual: se comió, se bebió — completely, all up.

5.2. Further reading — la reunión del consorcio

Context: The building association meets to decide on a new gate. Read it quietly:

El consorcio se reúne el primer lunes de cada mes en el zaguán.

The building association meets on the first Monday of every month in the entrance hall.

Quieren poner otra reja en la puerta de la escalera.

They want to put another iron gate on the stairwell door.

Se piden ideas a todos los vecinos antes de decidir nada.

Ideas are requested from all the neighbours before deciding anything.

Las llaves de la azotea las tiene el conserje.

The caretaker holds the roof-terrace keys.

En esta vecindad todos se conocen, y nadie se queja dos veces de lo mismo.

In this block everyone knows each other, and nobody complains twice about the same thing.

New words: consorcio (building association), reja (iron gate) — extension and core words from today's table.
