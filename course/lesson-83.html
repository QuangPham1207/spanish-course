LESSON 83 — OPINION COLUMN: WHOSE CITY IS IT?

Lesson goals:
  • Read an opinion column with light gloss: find the postura before the details.
  • Learn stance words: a mi juicio, es evidente que, matiz, réplica.
  • Notice concession: si bien, aunque — how columnists grant and strike.
  • Debate: the car or the pedestrian — Bogotá, Barcelona, Mexico City.

Core vocabulary: postura, argumento, matiz, réplica, a mi juicio, es evidente que, no es que… sino que…, cabe preguntarse, peatonalización, carril bici, ciclovía, calzada, estacionar, hora punta, disuasorio, convivir, ceder el paso, paso de cebra (plus known words acera, peatón, atasco, multa)
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): editorialista, tribuna, publirreportaje, línea editorial

1. READING PASSAGE (¿DE QUIÉN ES LA CIUDAD?)

Context: An opinion column from a Bogotá paper, the kind you read over coffee. Light gloss: the stance markers are bolded; the rest you guess. First question always: what is the author's postura?

(Bold words are in the vocabulary table. The English is only a check.)

A mi juicio, la pregunta está mal hecha: no es el coche contra el peatón, es la ciudad contra el atasco.

In my judgment, the question is wrongly put: it is not car versus pedestrian, it is the city versus the jam.

Es evidente que el coche ganó el siglo XX: calles anchas, prisa estrecha.

It is evident that the car won the 20th century: wide streets, narrow hurry.

Si bien nadie quiere prohibir el coche, cabe preguntarse a qué hora la ciudad es de quien camina.

Although nobody wants to ban the car, one may ask at what hour the city belongs to those who walk.

Mi postura es clara: peatonalización con matices, no con pancartas.

My stance is clear: pedestrianisation with nuances, not with banners.

El primer argumento es de salud: en hora punta el aire pica, el ruido cansa, el niño tose.

The first argument is about health: at rush hour the air stings, noise tires, children cough.

El segundo es de barrio: donde hay carril bici —ciclovía en América— y acera ancha, vuelve el café, vuelve el juego.

The second is about neighbourhoods: where there is a bike lane and a wide pavement, cafés return, play returns.

No es que el conductor sea el enemigo, sino que el diseño lo obliga a serlo.

It is not that drivers are the enemy, but that design forces them to be.

Aunque la multa educa, el parking disuasorio convence: deja el coche y entra andando.

Although fines teach, park-and-ride convinces: leave the car and walk in.

Anticipo la réplica: «¿Y el repartidor, y la abuela, y la lluvia?» Convivir es ceder el paso.

I anticipate the reply: "What about delivery riders, grandmothers, rain?" Living together means yielding.

El paso de cebra lo dice todo: rayas blancas donde el débil manda y el fuerte frena.

The zebra crossing says it all: white stripes where the weak command and the strong brake.

Convivir, en fin, no es prohibir: es repartir la calzada por horas, por usos, por turnos.

Living together, in short, is not banning: it is sharing out the street by hours, uses, turns.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. Stance markers: where the author stands

Opinion Spanish signs its position: a mi juicio (I judge), es evidente que (+ indicative, it claims fact), cabe preguntarse (one may ask — polite challenge). Learn them as blocks.

  • A mi juicio, la pregunta está mal hecha.In my judgment, the question is wrongly put.
  • Es evidente que el coche ganó el siglo XX.It is evident that the car won the 20th century.
  • Cabe preguntarse de quién es la calle a las ocho.One may ask whose the street is at eight.

2.2. Concede and strike: si bien, aunque, no es que… sino que

Columnists grant a point, then take it back: si bien (formal although), aunque + subjunctive for conceded ideas, and the correction frame no es que… sino que… (it's not that… but that…). (You met si bien in Lesson 73 and aunque in Lesson 37.)

  • Si bien nadie quiere prohibir el coche, la calle pide aire.Although nobody wants to ban cars, the street asks for air.
  • Aunque la multa eduque, el parking disuasorio convence.Although fines may teach, park-and-ride convinces.
  • No es que el conductor sea el enemigo, sino que el diseño lo obliga.It's not that drivers are the enemy, but that design forces them.
postura → argumento 1, 2 → réplica anticipada → convivir / ceder

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences to read once. No need to memorize.

1. The stance:

A mi juicio, la pregunta está mal hecha.

In my judgment, the question is wrongly put.

2. The fact-claim:

Es evidente que el coche ganó el siglo XX.

It is evident that the car won the 20th century.

3. The correction:

No es que el conductor sea el enemigo, sino que el diseño lo obliga.

It's not that drivers are the enemy, but that design forces them.

4. The reply:

Anticipo la réplica: ¿y la lluvia, y la abuela?

I anticipate the reply: what about rain, grandmothers?

5. The close:

Convivir no es prohibir: es repartir la calle.

Living together is not banning: it is sharing the street.

💡 Optional (skip if you like): read the five once. They carry every column you'll ever read.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: A reader argues with the column over coffee. No questions — just read.

Lector: Dice que no es el coche contra el peatón… ¿Cuál es su postura?

Reader: It says it's not car versus pedestrian… What is his stance?

Amiga: Peatonalización con matices: ni prohibir todo ni permitir todo.

Friend: Pedestrianisation with nuances: neither ban all nor allow all.

Lector: Si bien el aire en hora punta pica, yo vivo lejos…

Reader: Although rush-hour air stings, I live far away…

Amiga: Por eso pide parkings disuasorios: dejas el coche y cedes el paso.

Friend: That's why he asks for park-and-ride: you leave the car and yield.

Lector: No es que esté en contra, sino que… me convenció a medias.

Reader: It's not that I'm against it, but… he half-convinced me.

Amiga: Pues convivir es eso: repartir la calle por turnos.

Friend: Well, living together is that: sharing the street in turns.

New words in the dialogue: none — all from today's table or earlier lessons. Notice the reader borrowing the column's frames (si bien, no es que… sino que).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Stance first: a mi juicio, es evidente que, cabe preguntarse — read them before the facts.
  • Concede and strike: si bien, aunque, no es que… sino que….
  • City words: peatonalización, carril bici / ciclovía, hora punta / pico, atasco / trancón, paso de cebra — neutral Spanish with notes.

5.2. Further reading — la última línea

Context: The column's closing paragraph, lightly glossed. Read the stance, skip the rest:

A mi juicio, la ciudad del futuro se decide en la acera: un metro más para caminar, un coche menos para aparcar —estacionar en América—.

In my judgment, the future city is decided on the pavement: one more metre to walk, one less car to park.

Si bien el cambio molesta, el aire de mañana se vota hoy, cediendo el paso.

Although change annoys, tomorrow's air is voted today, by yielding.

All words are from today's table. Two sentences, one postura: did you catch it de un vistazo?
