LESSON 85 — SHORT STORY: THE HOUSE ON THE HILL

Lesson goals:
  • Read a story excerpt, semi-glossed: longer paragraphs, only story words bolded.
  • Learn story words: desván, mecedora, crujir, susurrar.
  • Hear the narrator's tenses: imperfect for the scene, preterite for the knock.
  • Meet Alba, who returns to her grandmother's house twenty years later.

Core vocabulary: desván, mecedora, crujir, susurrar, parpadear, postigo, pasillo, susurro, antaño, penumbra, telaraña, baúl, carta amarillenta, solapa, escalón, pasamanos, aldaba, umbral, desempolvar (plus known words lejanía, polvo, remitente, hojear)
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): narrador omnisciente, flashback, elipsis, clímax

1. READING PASSAGE (LA CASA DE LA COLINA, I)

Context: Alba returns to the village house where her grandmother raised her. Semi-glossed: each block is a small paragraph now, not one sentence. Guess the rest; the bold words carry the scene.

(Bold words are in the vocabulary table. The English is only a check — read the Spanish first.)

La casa seguía en la colina, antaño blanca, ahora del color de la lejanía. Alba empujó la puerta y el aire olió a polvo y a lluvia vieja.

The house still stood on the hill, once white, now the colour of distance. Alba pushed the door and the air smelled of dust and old rain.

En la penumbra del recibidor, la mecedora dormía junto a la ventana, bajo el postigo entornado. Alba la tocó y la madera empezó a crujir, como si despertara.

In the half-light of the hall, the rocking chair slept by the window, under the half-closed shutter. Alba touched it and the wood began to creak, as if waking.

Subió al desván por la escalera estrecha, agarrada al pasamanos, contando los escalones como de niña: siete.

She climbed to the attic up the narrow stair, holding the handrail, counting the steps as a child: seven.

Arriba la esperaba el baúl: una telaraña en la esquina, un candado abierto, un olor a alcanfor. Lo desempolvó con la manga.

Above, the trunk waited: a cobweb in the corner, an open padlock, a smell of mothballs. She dusted it with her sleeve.

Dentro había cartas amarillentas, atadas con una cinta azul. Alba hojeó la primera sin abrirla: ni remitente ni sello, solo su nombre en la solapa.

Inside were yellowed letters, tied with a blue ribbon. Alba flipped through the first without opening it: no sender, no stamp, only her name on the flap.

—Alba —susurró la casa, o eso creyó ella. La bombilla parpadeó y abajo golpeó la aldaba: dos veces.

"Alba," the house whispered — or so she believed. The bulb flickered and below the knocker struck: twice.

Bajó despacio, con la carta en la mano, y se detuvo en el umbral: la puerta ya estaba abierta.

She went down slowly, letter in hand, and stopped on the threshold: the door was already open.

En el pasillo quedó un susurro, como de pasos que se alejan.

In the corridor a whisper lingered, like footsteps moving away.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. The two pasts at work: scene vs knock

Literature lives on the contrast you learned in Lesson 18: the imperfect (seguía, dormía) holds the background; the preterite (empujó, crujió, golpeó) strikes the event. Stories are that alternation.

  • La casa seguía en la colina. Alba empujó la puerta.The house still stood on the hill. Alba pushed the door.
  • La mecedora dormía. La madera empezó a crujir.The chair slept. The wood began to creak.
  • La bombilla parpadeaba y la aldaba golpeó dos veces.The bulb was flickering and the knocker struck twice.

2.2. Dialogue tags: susurrar, decir, creer

Stories rarely just decir: they susurrar (whisper) first, and the tag tells you how the room feels. And free indirect thought (o eso creyó ella) blurs narrator and mind — the signature of literary voice.

  • —Alba —susurró la casa, o eso creyó ella."Alba," the house whispered — or so she believed.
  • Contó los escalones como de niña: siete.She counted the steps as a child: seven.
  • La puerta ya estaba abierta.The door was already open.
imperfecto (escena) → pretérito (golpe) → etiqueta (susurró) → umbral

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Alba calls her brother from the doorway. No questions — just read.

Alba: Estoy en el umbral… La puerta ya estaba abierta.

Alba: I'm on the threshold… The door was already open.

Hermano: No subas al desván sola. Espera a mañana.

Brother: Don't go up to the attic alone. Wait until tomorrow.

Alba: Ya subí. El baúl tiene cartas con mi nombre en la solapa.

Alba: I already went up. The trunk holds letters with my name on the flap.

Hermano: Ni las abras… ¿Oyes eso? ¿Cruje la mecedora?

Brother: Don't even open them… Do you hear that? Is the chair creaking?

Alba: Es el viento en la lejanía. O eso creo yo.

Alba: It's the wind in the distance. Or so I believe.

Hermano: Alba… Acabo de golpear la aldaba. Dos veces. Abre.

Brother: Alba… I just knocked. Twice. Open up.

New words in the dialogue: none — all from today's table or earlier lessons. Notice the story's tags living in speech (umbral, desván, solapa, crujir, aldaba) and the free-indirect echo (o eso creo yo).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Story words: desván, mecedora, crujir, susurrar, baúl, aldaba, umbral — the furniture of suspense.
  • Literary tenses: imperfecto (seguía, dormía) paints; pretérito (empujó, golpeó) knocks.
  • Voice: susurró… o eso creyó ella — narrator and mind in one line.

5.2. Further reading — la carta (semi-glossed)

Context: Alba opens the first letter. Only two words are glossed — guess the rest:

Querida Alba: si lees esto en el desván, con las manos llenas de polvo, es que ya volviste.

Dear Alba: if you are reading this in the attic, hands full of dust, you already came back.

No tengas miedo de la mecedora que cruje ni de la aldaba que golpea: son la casa dándote la bienvenida.

Don't fear the creaking chair or the knocking knocker: it is the house welcoming you.

Quédate esta noche. Duerme abajo, deja la puerta abierta. La colina cuida a los suyos.

Stay tonight. Sleep downstairs, leave the door open. The hill looks after its own.

Almost unglossed — only desván is marked (the dust, polvo, you know since L61). This is the Ladder's promise: soon, no bold at all.
