LESSON 35 — THE PRESENT SUBJUNCTIVE: WISHES & DOUBT

Lesson goals:
  • Understand what the subjunctive is and when Spanish uses it.
  • Form the present subjunctive: -ar verbs take -e, -er/-ir take -a.
  • Use it after wishes and doubt: quiero que…, ojalá, quizá, no creo que….
  • Pick up words: ojalá, quizá, esperar, lástima, sueño, meta.

Core vocabulary: universidad, economía, ojalá, quizá / quizás, tal vez, mejorar, lástima, emigrar, emigrante, solución, valer la pena, generación, dudar, cumplirse, sueño, deseo, meta, ilusión, pobreza, riqueza, desear, tanta/tanto, juventud, crisis, suerte, seguramente
Extension words (read only, no need to memorize): digna, intentar, entre

1. READING PASSAGE (LO QUE ESPERO PARA MI PAÍS)

Context: A young woman writes about her hopes for her country. Read each line; the English below it is only a check.

(The subjunctive appears every time she expresses a wish, a hope or a doubt.)

Quiero que mis hijos estudien en la universidad.

I want my children to study at university.

Espero que este año mejore la economía del país.

I hope the country's economy improves this year.

Ojalá llueva pronto, porque el campo lo necesita.

I hope it rains soon, because the countryside needs it.

Quizá tengamos que mudarnos de ciudad para encontrar trabajo.

Maybe we'll have to move to another city to find work.

No creo que sea fácil, pero no perdemos la esperanza.

I don't think it's easy, but we don't lose hope.

Mientras haya salud, todo lo demás se puede resolver.

As long as there is health, everything else can be solved.

Es una lástima que tanta gente joven emigre.

It's a shame that so many young people emigrate.

Mi gran sueño es una vida digna para todos.

My great dream is a dignified life for everyone.

💡 Notice: compare Quiero viajar (I want to travel — one subject) with Quiero que viajes (I want you to travel — two subjects). Only the second needs the subjunctive, because it uses que + a new subject.

2. CORE VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR

2.1. What the subjunctive is

Spanish has two "moods" for verbs: the indicative (facts, what is real) and the subjunctive (wishes, doubt, emotion, what is not certain). The subjunctive is not a tense — it is a way of viewing the action.

  • Quiero viajar a Chile.I want to travel to Chile. (indicative — one subject: yo)
  • Quiero que viajes a Chile.I want you to travel to Chile. (subjunctive — two subjects, a wish about someone else)
subject A + verb of wish/doubt + que + subject B + verb in subjunctive

2.2. Forming the present subjunctive

Take the yo form of the present, drop -o, and add the "opposite" vowel: -ar verbs take -e; -er/-ir verbs take -a.

Pronounhablar (-ar)comer (-er)vivir (-ir)
yohablecomaviva
túhablescomasvivas
él / ella / ustedhablecomaviva

💡 Handy rule: the yo form and the él / usted form look the same (hable, coma, viva). A few common irregulars: sea (ser), esté (estar), haya (haber), vaya (ir), sepa (saber), dé (dar).

2.3. Wishes and doubt — the triggers

  • Quiero que estudien.I want them to study. (querer que)
  • Espero que mejore.I hope it improves. (esperar que)
  • Ojalá (que) llueva.I hope it rains. (ojalá)
  • Quizá / Tal vez tengamos que ir.Maybe we'll have to go. (quizá, tal vez)
  • No creo que sea fácil.I don't think it's easy. (no creer que)
  • Dudo que vengan hoy.I doubt they'll come today. (dudar que)
querer / esperar / ojalá / quizá / dudar + que + subjuntivo

2.4. Indicative or subjunctive? creer and a lo mejor

With creer, the affirmative keeps the indicative, but the negative switches to the subjunctive:

  • Creo que es fácil.I think it's easy. (indicative)
  • No creo que sea fácil.I don't think it's easy. (subjunctive)
  • A lo mejor viene mañana.Maybe he's coming tomorrow. (a lo mejor + indicative — an exception)
⚠️ Common mistake: after ojalá always use the subjunctive: Ojalá haga sol, not Ojalá hace sol.

3. GOLDEN PHRASES — READ FOR FAMILIARITY

Five sentences that trigger the subjunctive. Read them once; no need to memorize.

1. A wish about someone else:

Quiero que mis hijos estudien en la universidad.

I want my children to study at university.

2. A hope:

Espero que este año mejore la economía.

I hope the economy improves this year.

3. With ojalá:

Ojalá llueva pronto, porque el campo lo necesita.

I hope it rains soon, because the countryside needs it.

4. A doubt:

No creo que sea fácil, pero no perdemos la esperanza.

I don't think it's easy, but we don't lose hope.

5. With quizá:

Quizá tengamos que mudarnos de ciudad.

Maybe we'll have to move to another city.

4. SAMPLE DIALOGUE (READING)

Context: Two friends talk about their hopes for the year.

Rosa: ¿Qué esperas para este año?

Rosa: What do you hope for this year?

Tomás: Espero que todo mejore y que mi familia esté bien.

Tomás: I hope everything improves and that my family is well.

Rosa: ¿Y tú, qué quieres?

Rosa: And you, what do you want?

Tomás: Quiero que mis hijos tengan un futuro mejor.

Tomás: I want my children to have a better future.

Rosa: Ojalá se cumpla.

Rosa: I hope it comes true.

New words in the dialogue: todo (everything), mejor (better), futuro (future).

5. SUMMARY & FURTHER READING

5.1. Lesson recap

  • Subjunctive = the mood of wishes, doubt and emotion; it appears after que with a new subject.
  • Endings: -ar → e, -er/-ir → a.
  • Triggers: querer que, esperar que, ojalá, quizá, tal vez, dudar que; no creo que + subj.
  • Exception: a lo mejor takes the indicative.

5.2. Further reading — la próxima generación

Context: A short note of hope. Read it quietly:

Ojalá encontremos una solución entre todos.

I hope we find a solution together.

Quizá sea difícil, pero vale la pena intentarlo.

Maybe it will be difficult, but it is worth trying.

Espero que la próxima generación viva con más oportunidades.

I hope the next generation lives with more opportunities.

No dudo que la juventud quiera quedarse en su país.

I don't doubt that young people want to stay in their country.

New words: entre (among, between), intentar (to try).
