# SPANISH COURSE SYLLABUS — A0 → B2 (READING)

Goal: take the learner from **absolute beginner (A0)** to **CEFR B2 reading
ability** with a **reading-first** method. All explanations are in English; the
target language is Spanish. Standard is **neutral Spanish**; where Spain and
Latin America differ, note both.

Method: build vocabulary, grammar and cultural knowledge **through reading**.
No homework, no tests, no submissions. Longer, real texts as the level rises.

**Scope honesty:** this course trains vocabulary, grammar and reading
comprehension. It can bring the learner to genuine **B2 reading** (the input
skills). Listening, speaking and writing are practiced elsewhere and are not
drilled here.

Progression of content:
- **A1-A2**: everyday daily-life topics (greetings, food, family, city, work,
  travel, weather, health, feelings).
- **B1**: society, history and culture of the Spanish-speaking world.
- **B2**: philosophy, politics, art, science and longer argumentative/cultural
  texts.
- **Reading ladder**: a final stage of progressively longer, increasingly
  unglossed authentic texts.

Scale: **90 teaching lessons + 14 re-reading reviews (≈104 files)**, 2 per week
≈ 11-12 months. Each module ends with one consolidated **review lesson by
re-reading** (never a test).

Legend: `(adv.)` = above the current level, read for exposure only.

---

## VOCABULARY & READING TARGETS (WHY THIS REACHES B2)

B2 reading comfort needs roughly **4,000-5,000 word families** (~8,000-10,000
lexical items) to cover ~95-98% of authentic text, plus large reading volume.
The curriculum is therefore built around explicit budgets:

- **New items per lesson**: ~20-25 at A1-A2, ~35 at B1, ~45-50 at B2.
- **Cumulative target**: ~700 items by end of A2, ~2,000 by end of B1,
  ~5,000 by end of B2.
- **Word families, not singletons**: record related forms (e.g. `nación →
  nacional → nacionalidad → nacionalizar`) so the JSON total reflects real
  coverage.
- **Beyond single words**: from B1 upward every module includes collocations,
  idioms, discourse markers and formal/academic or literary lexicon — the
  vocabulary real articles actually use.
- **Reading volume**: each lesson's passage grows from ~120-250 words (A1-A2)
  to 600-1,000+ words (B2); the Reading Ladder adds long unglossed texts.

The per-lesson JSONs remain the source of truth (`AGENTS.md` §8); `vocab.mjs`
tracks totals against these targets.

## LEVEL A1 — SURVIVAL & EVERYDAY BASICS
### MODULE A1-1: First contact
- **L01 — Greetings & the Spanish sounds.** *hola, buenos días, gracias, me
  llamo, ¿cómo estás?*; silent `h`, `ñ`, `ll`, `j`; stress & accent marks;
  `tú` vs `usted`.
- **L02 — Introducing yourself.** `ser`, `llamarse`, nationality and origin
  (`soy de…`), *encantado/a*.
- **L03 — Numbers, age & dates.** 0-100, `tener … años`, days, months.
- **L04 — Family & people.** Possessives (`mi, tu, su`), `tener`, family words.
- **L05 — Nouns: gender, articles & plurals.** `el/la`, `un/una`, `-s/-es`;
  exceptions.
- **Review A1-1** — re-read a first-contact dialogue.

### MODULE A1-2: Everyday life
- **L06 — The present tense.** Regular `-ar / -er / -ir` verbs, subject
  pronouns.
- **L07 — Food & ordering.** `querer, comer, beber`; café, market, restaurant;
  tapas vs antojitos (cultural note).
- **L08 — Home & daily routine.** Reflexive verbs (`levantarse, ducharse`);
  telling time.
- **L09 — Shopping & prices.** Numbers in use, `¿cuánto cuesta?`, colors,
  clothes.
- **L10 — Stem-changing verbs.** `e→ie`, `o→ue`, `e→i` (`querer, poder,
  pedir`); `ir`, `jugar`; verbs that work in daily life.
- **Review A1-2** — re-read a market scene.

### MODULE A1-3: Getting around
- **L11 — The city & directions.** `estar`, `hay`, `ir a`, prepositions of
  place.
- **L12 — Transport & travel basics.** `ir/tomar`, tickets, timetables.
- **L13 — Weather & seasons.** `hace frío/calor`, `llueve`, months and
  climates of Spain vs Latin America.
- **L14 — Likes & hobbies.** `gustar` / `encantar`, sports, music, free time.
- **L15 — A1 consolidation.** A longer beginner reading tying A1 together.
- **Review A1** — re-read an A1 dialogue + short text.

## LEVEL A2 — DAILY LIFE, STORIES & PLANS
### MODULE A2-1: Talking about the past
- **L16 — Past simple (pretérito indefinido), regular.**
- **L17 — Past simple, irregular.** *fui, tuve, hice, estuve…*
- **L18 — The imperfect & contrast with the preterite.** Background vs events.
- **L19 — Narrating a trip or story.** Putting the two pasts together.
- **L20 — The present perfect.** `he/has/ha…` + participle; contrasts with the
  preterite (Spain vs Latin America usage).
- **Review A2-1** — re-read a travel story.

### MODULE A2-2: Plans, needs & feelings
- **L21 — Near future & the simple future.** `ir a + inf.`, `-é/-ás…`.
- **L22 — Health & the body.** Doctor, symptoms, advice.
- **L23 — Opinions & feelings.** `creer, pensar, parecer, sentir`.
- **L24 — Commands for instructions.** Recipes and directions (`imperativo`).
- **L25 — Object pronouns & `gustar`-type verbs.** `lo/la/los/las`,
  `me/te/le…`, `doler, faltar, interesar, importar`.
- **Review A2-2** — re-read a recipe + an advice column.

### MODULE A2-3: Everyday culture of the Hispanic world
- **L26 — Comparatives & superlatives.** `más/menos … que`, `tan … como`,
  irregulars (`mejor, peor`).
- **L27 — `por` vs `para` (intro).** Core contrasts in everyday contexts.
- **L28 — Food culture.** Paella, tacos, arepas, asado; regional differences.
- **L29 — Festivals.** La Tomatina, Día de los Muertos, Inti Raymi.
- **L30 — Music & dance.** Flamenco, salsa, tango, cumbia.
- **L31 — Sport & football culture.** Clubs, rivalries, the street game.
- **L32 — A2 consolidation.** A longer A2 reading tying the level together.
- **Review A2** — re-read a culture article (A2).

## LEVEL B1 — SOCIETY, HISTORY & CULTURE
### MODULE B1-1: Grammar bridge I
- **L33 — The pluperfect & the past-tense timeline.** `había hecho`; ordering
  events across tenses.
- **L34 — Relative clauses.** `que, quien, el cual, cuyo, lo que`.
- **L35 — Present subjunctive (intro).** Wishes, doubt, `ojalá, quizá`.
- **L36 — Subjunctive with emotions & impersonal expressions.**
- **L37 — Subjunctive in adverbial clauses.** `para que, antes de que, a menos
  que, sin que, cuando + subj.,` `aunque`.
- **L38 — Subjunctive in adjective clauses.** `busco algo que…`, `no hay nadie
  que…`.
- **Review B1-1** — re-read short news/opinion pieces.

### MODULE B1-2: Grammar bridge II
- **L39 — The conditional & `si`-clauses.** Real and unreal conditions (types
  1-2).
- **L40 — Compound tenses.** Future perfect (`habré hecho`) and conditional
  perfect (`habría hecho`).
- **L41 — The passive voice.** `ser` + participle in all tenses; passive vs
  impersonal `se`.
- **L42 — Reported speech.** Statements, questions and tense backshift.
- **L43 — `por` vs `para` (full).** All the core uses, with reading examples.
- **L44 — Verb periphrases & aspect.** `acabar de, volver a, ponerse a, dejar
  de, seguir/llevar + gerundio, ir + gerundio, tener + pp`.
- **Review B1-2** — re-read an advanced grammar-in-context text.

### MODULE B1-3: History of the Spanish-speaking world
- **L45 — Al-Ándalus & medieval Spain.**
- **L46 — 1492: the Reconquista, expulsion, Columbus.**
- **L47 — Conquest & colonial Latin America.**
- **L48 — Independence: Bolívar, San Martín, Hidalgo.**
- **L49 — 20th-century Spain: Civil War & Franco.**
- **L50 — 20th-century Latin America: revolutions & dictatorships.**
- **Review B1-3** — re-read a history feature.

### MODULE B1-4: Culture & ideas
- **L51 — Art: Velázquez, Goya, Picasso, Kahlo, Rivera.**
- **L52 — Literature: Cervantes & *Don Quijote*.**
- **L53 — The Latin American Boom.** García Márquez, Borges, Cortázar.
- **L54 — Cinema & television.** Almodóvar, del Toro, Cuarón.
- **L55 — Music & contemporary culture.** From flamenco to reggaetón.
- **L56 — Language & identity (intro).** Regional varieties, voseo, indigenous

- **Review B1** — re-read a cultural essay (B1).

## LEVEL B2 — PHILOSOPHY, POLITICS & LONG READING
### MODULE B2-1: Advanced grammar & nuance
- **L57 — Imperfect subjunctive & tense sequences.** `si` + subjunctive.
- **L58 — Unreal & mixed conditionals.** `si tuviera…`, `si hubiera… habría…`.
- **L59 — The pluperfect subjunctive in all uses.** After `como si`, `ojalá`,
  past reporting.
- **L60 — The many values of `se`.** Reflexive, reciprocal, passive,
  impersonal, accidental and aspectual `se`.
- **L61 — Non-finite clauses.** Absolute participle constructions
  (`Terminada la guerra…`), infinitive-as-noun, gerund clauses.
- **L62 — Nominalization & formal written syntax.** `el aumento de`, `la
  implementación de` — the signature of written B2 Spanish.
- **L63 — Word formation I.** Prefixes and suffixes for decoding unknown words.
- **L64 — Word formation II.** Word families: `-ción, -dad, -mente, -ísimo`,
  augmentatives and diminutives.
- **L65 — Discourse connectors & register.** *sin embargo, no obstante,
  asimismo, por consiguiente, mientras que, a pesar de, dado que…*
- **L66 — Advanced pronouns.** Pronoun clusters (`se lo`), neuter `lo`, `el/la
  cual`, leísmo.
- **Review B2-1** — re-read an advanced grammar-in-context text.

### MODULE B2-2: Thought & identity

- **L71 — Art & aesthetics.** Avant-garde, muralism, contemporary art.
- **L72 — Literature & memory.** Dictatorship novels, *testimonio*, exile.
- **Review B2-2** — re-read a philosophical essay (B2).

### MODULE B2-3: The contemporary Hispanic world
- **L73 — Democracy & politics in Spain & Latin America.**
- **L74 — Economy, migration & inequality.**
- **L75 — Climate & environment across the Hispanic world.**
- **L76 — Media, technology & disinformation.**
- **L77 — Gender, feminism & social change.**
- **L78 — Science & health in the Spanish-speaking world.**
- **L79 — Global Hispanic.** Diaspora, Spanish in the United States.

- **Review B2-3** — re-read a contemporary-world feature.

## LEVEL B2+ — THE READING LADDER (AUTHENTIC TEXTS)
A skill-building finale: the same themes, but the glossing fades until the
learner reads unglossed texts with a dictionary, as at real B2.

- **L81 — Reading strategies.** Guessing from context, cognates, word families,
  and how to use a dictionary efficiently.
- **L82 — News report (glossed).** Straight reporting; who/what/when/where.
- **L83 — Opinion column (light gloss).** Recognising stance and argument.
- **L84 — Cultural essay (light gloss).** Longer paragraphs, abstraction.
- **L85 — Short story excerpt (semi-glossed).** Literary tenses and voice.
- **L86 — Historical feature (semi-glossed).** Dense narrative and dates.
- **L87 — Interview / profile (semi-glossed).** Direct speech and reported
  speech together.
- **L88 — Formal & academic writing (semi-glossed).** Abstract, report,
  official letter.
- **L89 — Full authentic article (unglossed).** Dictionary only; all strategies.
- **L90 — Capstone (unglossed).** A full short story or long essay, as at B2.
- **Review B2 / Final** — re-read the capstone + consolidated vocabulary.

## DESIGN NOTES (for the tutor / author)
- Every lesson follows the 5-part structure in `AGENTS.md` §5 and is a single
  HTML file modeled on `course/lesson-01.html`.
- Vocabulary and grammar must respect the level: do not use the subjunctive
  before L35 or the past tenses before L16, etc. Label out-of-level material
  `(advanced)`.
- Two-tier format everywhere: Spanish line + English meaning. From B2 (L57
  onward) known words may be left unglossed to encourage extensive reading,
  but the `AGENTS.md` contract keeps the two-tier block by default; the Reading
  Ladder is where glossing deliberately fades.
- At B1+, replace generic topics with real stories, specific people and
  present-day debates. Prefer the less-obvious angle.
- Every B1+ module should weave in collocations, idioms and connectors, not
  only single words; record word families in the JSON (`AGENTS.md` §8.3).
- Review lessons are **re-reading** consolidations: no questions, no grading.
- Numbers (L01…L90) are a guide, not a cage. Add or split lessons when a topic
  needs it, and keep `course/data/lesson-XX.json` in sync.
- `L01-L03` are already built and unchanged; the renumbering starts to bite
  from the new L10 (stem-changing verbs) onward.
